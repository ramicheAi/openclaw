# Immigration Paralegal Call — Field Guide

A one-glance companion to `Themis-Explainer-and-Questions.pdf`. That PDF is what you
*hand her / walk her through*. This is what *you* keep open during the call. Glance, don't read.

---

## The one job of this call
**Learn her week, not pitch your app.** You already know Themis is good. What you don't know is
which 3 frustrations in *her* actual day are worth building for. Talk ~30%, listen ~70%.
Every frustration she names is a feature. Don't promise any feature live (see the trap below).

**The single most valuable outcome** isn't a sale — it's: *"Can I set you up with one of your
real (or a sanitized) cases and watch you use it for 30 minutes?"* That watch session is worth
more than anything she'll say in the interview.

---

## The 5 must-ask questions + what each answer is really telling you

1. **"Walk me through one case from intake to filing."**
   → *Decoder:* the order she lists steps = the order your tabs should appear. Note where she
   says "then I wait for…" — those waits are where deadlines/reminders earn their keep.

2. **"What eats the most hours and feels most repetitive?"**
   → *Decoder:* this is the wedge. If it's *assembling the record / index*, that's Documents +
   Chronology — already your core. If it's *RFE responses* or *declarations*, that's Draft Studio
   and you ship those templates first.

3. **"When you cite law — BIA decisions, the INA, circuit cases, regs — where do you look it up,
   and how do you confirm it's still good law?"**
   → *Decoder:* THE key technical question. Cite Check today only checks CourtListener (federal +
   state courts). **It does NOT cover BIA/AAO decisions or the INA statute** — that's the #1 build
   gap for immigration. Her answer tells you exactly which sources to add and whether she'd even
   trust an automated check here.

4. **"How much of your evidence is in other languages, and how do you handle translation +
   certification?"**
   → *Decoder:* immigration is translation-heavy in a way PI/litigation isn't. If she's drowning
   in this, OCR + a translation-certification tracker is a differentiator generic legal AI skips.

5. **"How do you track the same client across multiple petitions / family members?"**
   → *Decoder:* your data model is *one matter = one case*. Immigration clusters by family/sponsor
   (one principal + derivatives across I-130/I-485/asylum). If she nods hard here, you need a
   "family/sponsor cluster" view — a real structural change, worth knowing before you sell.

## Immigration-specific signals to confirm (you already half-built these — get her to validate)
- **Deadline language:** does she think in *RFE response dates, master/individual hearing dates,
  filing windows, priority dates* — not "scheduling order"? Your deadline parser must speak EOIR/
  USCIS, not litigation.
- **Government systems:** does she live in **USCIS online / EOIR ECAS / case-status tools**? Ask if
  they talk to her other software — tells you what to integrate vs. replace.
- **Country-conditions evidence:** is it a recurring, reusable shelf she rebuilds per case? If yes,
  a "country-conditions library" (save once, reuse across asylum matters) is a clean feature.
- **The trust question (ask it straight):** *"If a tool said it used AI on your cases, what would
  make you trust it — or refuse?"* Her answer is your entire trust-story script. Listen for whether
  the hash-chain/"never fabricates" angle lands as hard here as in litigation.

## The trap — do NOT do this
When she names a pain and you *already have the feature*, the urge is to jump in and demo. Resist
until the **watch session**. If you patch every gap verbally mid-interview, you stop learning and
you over-promise things that aren't immigration-ready yet (BIA citation checking literally isn't
built). Write the pain down. Say "that's really helpful" and move on.

## The close (say roughly this)
> "This was exactly what I needed — I'd rather build the right thing than guess. Two asks:
> (1) Can I set you up with one of your real cases, sanitized however you need, and just watch you
> use Themis for 30 minutes? I'll fix whatever trips you up. (2) If it ends up saving you real
> hours, I'd want you as one of a handful of paid design partners shaping the immigration version —
> at a founder price. And — who else in immigration should I be talking to?"

Paid design partner > free pilot: a paid yes is the only signal that means anything (Business
Bible — resourcefulness, the gasp anchor). Referrals are the cheapest growth you'll ever get.

## After the call — 48-hour checklist
- [ ] Write the **3 biggest frustrations** she named, verbatim, while fresh. Those rank the build.
- [ ] Note every **document type** she handles (I-589, I-130, RFE, NTA, country reports…) → tune
      extraction + which Draft templates ship first.
- [ ] Decide the **immigration Cite Check gap** scope: BIA/AAO + INA sources (confirm her answer to Q3).
- [ ] If she said yes to the watch session — **book it before you leave** (BAMFAM: book-a-meeting-
      from-a-meeting). Put a real date on it.
- [ ] Log who she referred; send the thank-you same day.
- [ ] Tell me what she said — I'll turn the top 3 frustrations into a scoped build plan and we
      sequence it against the security/hosting work.
