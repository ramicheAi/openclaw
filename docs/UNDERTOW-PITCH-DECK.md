## Slide 1 — Title

**UNDERTOW** (逆流 — Gyakuryū, "Countercurrent")

*A boy who fears water discovers he carries the ocean's rarest Current — and must swim
to its bottom to bring his father home.*

The first great swimming battle-anime. 24 × 22 minutes, season one of five.

> *Art: `poster-deep-calls-to-deep-4k.png` — 4K print master.*

## Slide 2 — The Gap

Swimming is the most universally practiced survival skill alive and one of the
most-watched Olympic sports — and it has **never had its battle anime.** Volleyball got
Haikyuu. Basketball got Slam Dunk. `Free!` took swimming as slice-of-life. Nobody has
claimed the water as an arena.

And water is anime's best canvas: **beautiful** (every great studio flexes on water),
**dangerous** (the arena itself can kill — stakes for free), **mythic** (every culture on
Earth has sea gods and flood legends), **universal** (everyone has felt water hold them
up and pull them down).

## Slide 3 — The Engine (built from six proven machines)

- **One Piece** → the declared dream + found-family crew
- **Naruto** → the outcast hero + the prodigy rival
- **Solo Leveling** → a visible, chartable power ladder
- **Attack on Titan** → the hidden world + buried-truth mystery
- **Baki** → reverent body-spectacle — with *real* freediving science as the lectures
- **Invincible** → a father-shaped reckoning, none of the gore

One synthesis: **real sport on the surface, myth in the deep.**

## Slide 4 — The World

Episode 1 is a normal school pool. By episode 12 the audience knows the truth: beneath
competitive swimming lies a hidden world — **ten bloodline Houses** descended from a
civilization that drowned itself 12,000 years ago, plus **an eleventh, unrecognized
Caribbean line** that has fought three centuries for a seat at the table. Drowned
world-trees whose roots seal a Gate at the bottom of the sea. A secret circuit, the
Meridian. Games held in sunken ruins.

The pool was the walls. The ocean is what's outside.

> *Art: `env-fathom-arena.png`, `env-port-royal.png`.*

## Slide 5 — The Power System (one card: see the Fathom Card)

Three layers, all legible:

- **Breath** — real freediving physiology (dive reflex, bradycardia). The floor; anyone
  can train it.
- **Current** — inherited resonance. The ocean's great currents are dragons, and
  awakening means one learns your name. Three laws, never broken: water only; amplifies
  technique, never replaces it; two Currents in one lane **interfere** — the duel.
- **Fathom** — the rank ladder, five tiers named for real ocean zones (Sunlit → Hadal).

Doctrine: *real at the surface, legend in the deep.* School meets stay sport-true;
Midnight-and-below goes full battle-anime — named techniques, domain-class Sovereign
Waters, myth-grade feats.

## Slide 6 — The Hero

**Kai Isozaki, 15.** Nearly drowned at seven chasing his father — the greatest
open-water swimmer alive — into a night sea. Terrified of water ever since; the school
calls him "Anchor." Forced back into a pool, he sinks… and doesn't drown: **4:07 on a
stopwatch**, calm, heart at 34 BPM. The extinct Undertow line isn't extinct.

His dream, said out loud in episode 1: *"I'm going to swim to the bottom of the sea and
bring my dad home."*

## Slide 7 — The Cast

A crew of the written-off. **Bo "Buoy" Fujii (16)**, no bloodline, monstrous trained
Breath — proof work climbs. **Mirei Sanda (15)**, the tactician who reads races like
sheet music. **Tsukiko "Luna" Amane (16)**, unregistered free-diver caught between old
ways and institutions, who teaches Kai to breathe. **Ren Kurose (16)**, the prodigy heir
whose Current awakened shallow — the rival mirror. **Coach Gouda (44)**, Olympic legend
turned wrecked coach who once had to put down his own drowned best friend. **Kemar
"Riddim" Reid (17)** of the unrecognized Freewater House, who swims to a beat only he
hears, and is the first to call Kai a brother.

Every heritage on Earth is in these lanes — unannounced, the way real pools are.

> *Art: `cast-lineup-sheet.png` (true height chart), `crew-key-visual.png`.*

## Slide 8 — The Villain

**Kaien Nakaru, 38.** The hidden world's institutions detected the tsunami that killed
his family **forty minutes early** — and said nothing, to protect their secrecy. He is
not wrong about the crime; he is wrong about the sentence: open the Gate and let the
sea's truth loose on everyone equally. The audience will spend seasons arguing about
him. That's the design.

The score agrees with him. He is not written as a monster — his motif is the show's own
theme, answered in the wrong key. Nothing detuned, nothing growling.

> *Art: `rivals-key-visual.png`.*

## Slide 9 — Season One (24 episodes, fully beat-sheeted)

- **Arc 1 (1–6):** a dying swim club and a boy who can't put his face in water — played
  warm and true.
- **Arc 2 (7–12):** the hidden lane; the first descent into a drowned ruin.
- **Arc 3 (13–19):** the Twilight Trials — training, first current-clash, a loss that
  matters, and a Breath-only teammate qualifying beside bloodlines.
- **Arc 4 (20–24):** the Second Tide strikes, the cover-up cracks — and a Warden's
  ledger surfaces, signed by Kai's father *three years after* his recorded drowning.

## Slide 10 — The Ending Exists

Five seasons, mapped. The finale is written down **now** — FMAB standard, not
make-it-up-later.

- **Eleven mysteries, ledgered**: every one has a planted episode, a payoff window, and
  its answer written in advance.
- **The ending spends a permanent price**: Kai's answer to the villain isn't victory,
  it's mercy that costs him something concrete — he binds part of his own gift into the
  seal, capping his own depth forever. He does not get his father back the way he asked
  for in episode one.
- **The last shot is planted in season one**: the first stroke Kai's father ever taught
  him, taught again, by Kai, to a scared kid on a pool deck.

Not concepts. Production sheets, on model, under a QC gate:

- **Eight leads with locked character elements** — Kai, Ren, Bo, Mirei, Luna, Gouda,
  Kemar, Nakaru — each with a neutral baseline plate, a **four-view turnaround**
  (front / three-quarter / profile / back on one ground line) and a **full expression
  sheet** (six to eight panels).
- **A cast height chart** composited from the approved plates with zero generation —
  all eight on one ground line, verified to within 0.5 cm.
- **Five key-art pieces**: the hero poster *Deep Calls to Deep* (plus alternate and a
  4K print master), the crew visual, the rivals visual.
- **Three environment keys**: the sunken Port Royal seat of the Freewater House; the
  Fathom Games arena as a drowned amphitheatre descending into a black shaft; the
  Episode 1 cold open — one lamp on a boat, one light far below, and the enormous black
  between them.
- **The palette is the power system**: chlorine cyan → green-gold → violet twilight →
  bioluminescent midnight → Hadal, where the only colours are what characters carry.
  Pause any frame and you know the depth, and therefore the stakes.

**The discipline behind it:** no character is ever generated from text. Every asset
carries a provenance chain to an approved identity, every file is checksummed, and a
commit hook blocks art that fails verification. **70 assets registered, every one signed
off.** What that buys a studio: a design package that cannot silently drift.

## Slide 12 — The Sound Is Written and Owned

A series is identified by its theme. If that theme is library music, the thing the
audience recognises belongs to someone else. So UNDERTOW's is composed from first
principles and **owned outright** — no library music, no sample packs, no cleared cues,
no generated audio, no licence attached to the sound of the show.

**The grammar** — the title phrase, Psalm 42:7, *deep calls to deep*, made literal:

- **THE CALL** — five notes that fall a sixth, then lift one step: sinking, and refusing
  to stay sunk. Singable on first hearing, which is the whole point.
- **THE ANSWER** — the same figure returned from below, an octave and a fifth down, two
  bars late, slower and heavier.

Once the audience knows the call is always answered, the score says what dialogue
cannot — and never explains itself:

| The answer arrives… | What it means |
|---|---|
| **on time** | the world is in order |
| **early** | the deep is reaching for someone |
| **in the wrong key** | the Second Tide. Nakaru, spoken or not |
| **not at all** | the worst thing in the show. Spend almost never |

**And the rank ladder is audible.** The five Fathom tiers spell the show's home chord
downward — D5 · A4 · F4 · D3 · D2. As you descend, treble dies and space grows, both
physically true of water, so nobody has to be told which sound is deeper. Hadal breaks
the pattern on purpose: it is the only cue with no attack. You do not hear yourself
arrive there.

**Delivered:** the 64-second main theme, five Fathom rank stingers and five character
motifs — a ten-piece signature kit, 24-bit/48 kHz, level-matched at −16 LUFS with a
−1.0 dBTP ceiling, so a music editor can drop any two cues on a timeline and hear them
matched. The design claims are machine-verified, not asserted: the ladder is confirmed
monotonically darker and more spacious across all five ranks; the dive-reflex motif
measurably slows; the Riddim Break contains a real 3.57-second hole.

## Slide 14 — Audience, and the Lane No Anime Has Ever Had

Four-quadrant by design: kids get adventure and a power ladder they can chart; adults
get grief, faith, and institutions worth arguing about (the dual-register table is in
the bible, thread by thread).

The Houses are rooted in real global sea-mythology — Japan, Greece, West Africa, the
Arctic, Polynesia, the North Atlantic, the Amazon, South Asia, East Asia — built to be
developed with tradition-keepers, not borrowed as aesthetic.

And one asset no battle anime has had: **the power system's buddy rules are the safety
rules.** The show never teaches a kid a trick that could kill them; dangerous water
behaviour always meets on-screen consequence or correction. That makes swim schools,
lifesaving federations and learn-to-swim charities natural partners — a standing
invitation, open and unclaimed. UNDERTOW can plausibly *teach kids to swim.*
