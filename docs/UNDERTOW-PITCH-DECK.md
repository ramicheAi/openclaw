## Slide 1 — Title

**UNDERTOW** (逆流 — Gyakuryū, "Countercurrent")

*A boy who nearly drowned — and has feared water ever since — discovers he carries the
rarest of the ocean's ancient Currents, and must swim from the last lane of a school
pool to the deepest trench on Earth to find the father the sea took from him.*

The first great swimming battle-anime.

## Slide 2 — The Gap

Swimming: the world's most-practiced survival skill, a top-three Olympic TV sport —
and it has **never had its battle anime.** Volleyball got Haikyuu. Basketball got
Slam Dunk. The water is still open.

And water is anime's best canvas: it is beautiful (every great studio flexes on water),
dangerous (the arena itself can kill — stakes for free), mythic (every culture on Earth
has sea gods and flood legends), and universal (everyone has felt water hold them up
and pull them down).

## Slide 3 — The Engine (built from five proven machines)

- **One Piece** → the declared dream + found-family crew
- **Naruto** → the outcast hero + the prodigy rival
- **Solo Leveling** → a visible, chartable power ladder
- **Attack on Titan** → the hidden world + buried-truth mystery
- **Baki** → reverent body-spectacle — with *real* freediving science as the lectures

One synthesis: **real sport on the surface, myth in the deep.**

## Slide 4 — The World

Episode 1 is a normal school pool. By episode 12 the audience knows the truth: beneath
competitive swimming lies a hidden world — **eleven bloodline Houses** descended from
a civilization that drowned itself 12,000 years ago, drowned world-trees whose roots
seal a Gate at the bottom of the sea, a secret circuit (the Meridian), and games held
in sunken ruins. The pool was the walls. The ocean is what's outside.

## Slide 5 — The Power System (one card: see the Fathom Card)

Three layers, all legible: **Breath** (real freediving physiology — anyone can train
it), **Current** (inherited resonance — the ocean's great currents are dragons, and
awakening means one learns your name), **Fathom** (the rank ladder, five tiers named
for real ocean zones). Doctrine: *real at the surface, legend in the deep* — school
meets stay sport-true; Midnight-and-below goes full battle-anime: named techniques,
domain-class Sovereign Waters, myth-grade feats.

## Slide 6 — The Hero

**Kai Isozaki, 15.** Nearly drowned at seven chasing his father — the greatest
open-water swimmer alive — into a night sea. Terrified of water ever since; the school
calls him "Anchor." Forced back into a pool, he sinks… and doesn't drown: four minutes
on the bottom, calm, heart at 34bpm. The extinct Undertow line isn't extinct.

His dream, said out loud in episode 1: *"I'm going to swim to the bottom of the sea,
and I'm going to bring my dad home."*

## Slide 7 — The Cast

A crew of the written-off: **Bo**, no bloodline, monstrous trained Breath — proof work
climbs; **Mirei**, the tactician who reads races like sheet music; **Luna**, ama-island
freediver caught between old ways and institutions; **Ren Kurose**, the prodigy heir
whose Current awakened "shallow" — the rival mirror; **Gouda**, Olympic legend turned
wrecked coach who's seen where the deep road ends; **Kemar "Riddim" Reid** of the
unrecognized Freewater House, who swims to a beat only he hears. Every heritage on
Earth is in these lanes — unannounced, the way real pools are.

## Slide 8 — The Villain

**Kaien Nakaru.** The hidden world's institutions detected the tsunami that killed his
family forty minutes early — and said nothing, to protect their secrecy. He is not
wrong about the crime; he is wrong about the sentence: open the Gate and let the sea's
truth loose on everyone equally. The audience will spend seasons arguing about him.
That's the design.

## Slide 9 — Season One (24 episodes, fully beat-sheeted)

Arc 1: a dying swim club and a boy who can't put his face in water — played warm and
true. Arc 2: the hidden lane; the first descent into a drowned ruin. Arc 3: the
Twilight Trials — training, first current-clash, a loss that matters. Arc 4: the
Second Tide strikes, the cover-up cracks — and a Warden's ledger surfaces, signed by
Kai's father *three years after* his recorded drowning.

## Slide 10 — The Ending Exists

Five seasons, mapped. The finale is written down now — FMAB standard, not
make-it-up-later: every mystery has its answer logged in advance, the ending spends a
permanent price, and the last shot of the series is the first stroke Kai's father ever
taught him — taught again, by Kai, to a scared kid on a pool deck.

## Slide 11 — Audience & the Lane No Anime Has Ever Had

Four-quadrant by design: kids get adventure and power scaling; adults get grief,
faith, and institutions worth arguing about (the dual-register table is in the bible).
And one unprecedented asset: the sport truth is real enough that **swim coaches and
water-safety organizations can endorse it** — the show never teaches a kid a trick
that could kill them. UNDERTOW can plausibly *teach kids to swim.* No battle anime has
ever had that partnership lane.
