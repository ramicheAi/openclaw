## 1. Trademark — search first, decide the name

**Do:** search "UNDERTOW" in the trademark databases for the classes that matter —
animation/film, comics/publications, and games/merch.
- US: USPTO search (search.uspto.gov, the TESS replacement).
- International: WIPO Global Brand Database (branddb.wipo.int).

**Expect collisions** — "undertow" is a common word (bands, films, products). What
matters is whether anything is registered *in entertainment/anime/comics* that would
block you. If the exact classes are crowded:
- Option A: keep UNDERTOW as the story title but trademark a **distinct logo lockup**
  (the wordmark + 逆流 + a mark) rather than the bare word.
- Option B: shift to a more ownable coined title and keep "Undertow" as a tagline.

## 2. Copyright — register the canon set (cheap, fast, dated)

Copyright exists the moment you write, but *registration* is what lets you enforce it
and sets a public date. Register the whole canon package as **one unpublished literary
work** (a "collection") so it's a single fee.

- US: eCO (copyright.gov) → "Literary Work" → unpublished collection.
- Title it something like *"UNDERTOW — Series Bible and Canon Documents, v2.0."*
- Bundle: bible, elements registry, lore codex, beat sheet, pilot script, codexes,
  visdev briefs, pitch deck, Fathom card. (Export them as one PDF — say the word and

## 4. Collaborator terms — never let anyone contribute unprotected

Every artist, writer, or animator signs *before* they make anything, in writing. Two
standard shapes:
- **Work-for-hire:** they're paid, you own 100% of what they make. (Default for
  commissioned art and pages.)
- **Co-ownership / revenue-share:** spell out the split and who controls canon. (Only
  for genuine creative partners, e.g. a mangaka building it with you.)

Non-negotiable clause in either: **you retain final approval over bible-level canon.**
Episode details are tradeable; the four pillars and the locked endgame are not.
