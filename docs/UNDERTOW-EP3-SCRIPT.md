## COLD OPEN (2:30)

**BLACK. A CLINICAL VOICE-OVER — unhurried, textbook-plain, the deliberate opposite
register of Grandmother's scripture V.O. in episode one:**

> **NARRATOR (V.O.):** Every air-breathing diving mammal shares it. Cold water touches
> the face — here, and here — and before the mind can decide anything, the body
> already has. Heart rate falls. Blood retreats from the limbs toward the chest,
> toward the organs that cannot go without. It is not fear. It is the oldest reflex
> a human being owns.

*Quick, gorgeous, documentary-plain cuts: a harbor seal's whiskered face breaking a
grey swell; a Bajau free-diver descending a rope hand under hand, utterly unhurried;
a laboratory heart-rate trace on an old CRT monitor, the line dropping in real time
as a gloved hand lowers a subject's face into a basin of ice water — 82… 61… 44.*

> **NARRATOR (V.O.) (CONT'D):** We call it the mammalian dive reflex. It does not
> need to be taught. It only needs to be *allowed.*

**EXT. HARBOR DOCK — DAWN**

*The imagery resolves into something ordinary: BO, in his family's oilskins, dunks
his face straight into a bucket of harbor water to rinse salt and net-slime off his
cheeks — no ceremony, no bravery, just a fisherman's kid doing a fisherman's chore.
His FATHER, a broad, sun-cured man, works the far end of the boat, near enough to
glance over without being asked to. Bo comes up dripping, shakes off like a dog,
goes back to hauling.*

*From the shadow of the harbor-master's shed, unseen, GOUDA watches — an old,
water-stained paperback in one hand, dog-eared past its spine: a coaching manual
older than most of his players. He looks at Bo. Looks down at a diagram on the open
page — the same trigeminal-nerve map we just saw in the cutaway. Looks back at Bo,
who has already forgotten the moment entirely and is singing badly to himself again.*

**GOUDA** *(very quiet, to no one)*: He's been doing it for free his whole life and
never once called it anything.

*He closes the book. The decision costs him something we can see in his shoulders —
this is the promise from episode two breaking, on schedule, exactly as his own face
told us it would.*

**TITLE CARD — EPISODE 3: STILL HEART**

## ACT ONE (6:00)

**INT. MUNAKATA HIGH SCHOOL — CLASSROOM — MONDAY MORNING**

*Homeroom. The TEACHER raps the board for attention.*

**TEACHER:** Before roll — we have a transfer. Please make him welcome.

*The door slides. REN KUROSE steps in: immaculate uniform worn like a dress
blazer, posture that makes the room's slouching teenagers look like a different
species. A ripple goes through the class — someone whispers "Kurose? *The*
Kurose?" — the kind of name-recognition a swim-mad school gives a sprint prodigy.*

**REN** *(flat, exact, giving away nothing)*: Ren Kurose. Transferring from Tokyo.
*(a beat, to the teacher, not the room)* Wherever's convenient.

*He is pointed to an open desk. Crossing the room, his eyes pass over the class
once, cataloguing — and land on Kai for exactly one beat. Cold. Assessing.
Unreadable. Kai, who has never seen this boy's face before in his life, feels the
look anyway, the way you feel a draft before you find the open window. Ren sits.
Opens a notebook. Does not look over again. Class resumes. Nothing is said. Nothing
needs to be, yet.*

**INT. HALLWAY — MOMENTS LATER**

*Bo catches up to Kai, already buzzing.*

**BO:** Did you hear the name? Kurose. There's a flyer in the sports office with his
splits on it from Nationals last spring. Kid's a *rocket.*

**KAI** *(distracted, still turning the look over in his head)*: …Yeah. I noticed.

**BO:** You okay? You've got your fence-face on.

**KAI:** I don't have a fence-face.

**BO** *(cheerfully unconvinced)*: You've absolutely got a fence-face.

*Kai doesn't argue further. Whatever he felt in that classroom, he can't name it
yet — so he lets Bo's noise carry them both down the hall instead.*

**INT. GYM — AFTER SCHOOL**

*The four-person club, assembled on the mats — a folding whiteboard wheeled in,
unusual. GOUDA stands beside it, the same water-stained paperback from the cold open
now flat on a table next to a plastic basin of water, a coil of tubing, and a small
clip-on pulse monitor with a digital readout. Mirei's chart-in-progress leans against
the wall behind him, mostly empty squares still.*

**GOUDA:** Sit down. This isn't drills. This is the lecture I've been putting off
since Friday, and if I put it off any longer it stops being caution and starts being
cowardice. *(beat)* So. The mammalian dive reflex.

*He draws it plain and fast on the whiteboard: a crude head, a nerve traced from the
face down toward the heart, an arrow labeled HEART RATE with a line going down, not
up.*

**GOUDA** *(cont'd):* Cold water touches your face — specifically here, this nerve —
and your body does three things without asking your permission. Heart rate drops.
Blood pulls in from your arms and legs toward your chest. Blood pressure in your core
climbs to compensate. *(tapping the diagram)* Every diving animal on Earth has a
stronger version of this. You have a real one. Untrained. Right now. All four of you.

**BO** *(hand up, delighted)*: Is that why I don't mind the bucket?

**GOUDA:** That's exactly why you don't mind the bucket. You've been training this by
accident for years hauling nets at dawn. *(a look Bo doesn't catch the weight of)* I
watched you do it this morning and didn't call it anything either, for a long time.
That was my mistake, not yours.

**MIREI** *(pen already moving, all business)*: So it's not willpower.

**GOUDA:** It's biology. Willpower is what you use *after* the reflex kicks in, to
stay calm instead of panicking against it. The reflex isn't the hard part. The hard
part was never courage. *(a beat, looking at Kai now, direct and careful)* It's
letting your body do a thing it already knows how to do.

*Kai says nothing. Something in his face has gone very still — not the pool's eerie
stillness, just a boy hearing, for the first time, that the worst thing about him
might have a name that isn't cowardice.*

**GOUDA** *(cont'd, business-like, moving them along)*: We're not taking anyone's
word for it. Basin's cold. One at a time, face in for a three-count, pulse clip on
first. Bo — go.

*Bo clips the monitor to his own finger, grins at the readout — 71 — dunks his face
in without ceremony, comes up at three seconds. The readout has dropped to 52.*

**BO** *(proud, dripping)*: Fifty-two! Gouda, that's a personal best for doing
nothing.

**MIREI** *(clipping the monitor on, prim)*: For science.

*She dunks, comes up composed, adjusts her glasses. 79 to 68. She notes it without
comment, though her eyes flick toward Kai's turn coming up.*

*Kai clips the monitor on. 94 — his baseline is already high, just from watching.
He hesitates over the basin exactly the length of a held breath, then puts his face
in.*

*Three seconds. He comes up. The readout: 94 to 51.*

*Silence. Gouda looks at the number a beat too long.*

**MIREI** *(quiet, to her own page, not quite to the room)*: That's not a normal
drop for a first exposure.

**GOUDA** *(covering, too quick)*: It's a good drop. That's all that number means
today. *(beat, gathering the room back)* Theory's easy. Basins are easy. Tomorrow
we find out if it holds water.

*Bo whoops. Kai looks at his own hand — the one that was just underwater — like it
belongs to someone slightly more capable than he thought he was an hour ago.*

## ACT TWO (6:30)

**EXT. POOL — LATE AFTERNOON, THE NEXT DAY**

*Gates open, lights on, the deck otherwise near-empty — deliberately scheduled after
regular hours, Gouda's attempt at containing the risk. It doesn't fully work: a
handful of stragglers have drifted to the fence anyway, the same reflexive audience
that gathers whenever the Anchor is rumored to be trying something. Among them,
unhurried, unannounced — REN, apparently just touring the facilities on his first
week, pausing at the fence like it means nothing. It might not. It might mean
everything. He says nothing, watches.*

*On the deck: Bo already in the water in the shallow end, waiting, calm as a held
door. Mirei on deck with the stopwatch and the pulse clip, wired to a small
readout she can see from dry land. Gouda crouches at the gutter's edge, level with
Kai, who stands in the shallow end up to his chest, borrowed jammers, the water lit
gold-green in the late sun.*

**GOUDA** *(steady, laying out the rule before anything else)*: Rule first, same as
always. Bo's arm's length from you the whole time. Mirei's got eyes on the clock and
the monitor. I don't move from this spot. Nobody goes under on this deck alone. Not
today, not ever, not on my watch.

**KAI** *(quiet)*: …Got it.

**GOUDA:** All you're doing is what you did in the basin. Face in, three-count,
face out. That's the whole ask. Nothing brave. Nothing fast.

*Kai nods. Bo gives him a small, steady nod back — the same "you're on a dock,
nothing's coming for you" energy from episode two, translated to water.*

*Kai breathes the way Bo taught him. Slow in. Slow out. Bends at the knees, brings
his face down toward the surface —*

*— and the moment his own reflection breaks apart under his nose, something behind
his eyes does not see chlorine and afternoon light. It sees moonlight in shafts.
Black water below. A vast, patient light going out.*

*He doesn't submerge. He convulses backward, chest locking exactly the way it did
on the block in episode one — except this time there is no trance waiting to catch
him. No stillness. No gathering quiet. Just a fifteen-year-old boy losing his
footing in three feet of water, thrashing, unable to find the bottom with feet that
know perfectly well where the bottom is.*

**BO** *(instantly there, grabbing for him)*: Kai — Kai, I've got you, stand up, the
floor's right there—

*Kai can't hear him. This is not the pool floor's cathedral hush from episode one.
This is only fear, at full, ordinary volume.*

*Gouda is in the water before he's decided to be, shoes and all, hauling Kai up and
out bodily onto the deck in one motion that costs him something in the back he'll
feel tomorrow. Kai comes up choking on water he never actually went under in.*

**MIREI** *(stopwatch frozen, staring at it like it lied to her a second time)*:
…Four seconds. He didn't even get his face wet the whole way.

*At the fence, the stragglers murmur — cruelty deflating into something closer to
awkward pity, worse in its own way. Ren watches the whole thing without a flicker,
produces a slim notebook from his jacket, writes one line, closes it, and walks
away before anyone thinks to notice him leaving.*

**EXT. POOL DECK — MOMENTS LATER**

*The fence has emptied. Kai sits wrapped in a towel, soaked despite never
submerging, staring at his own shaking hands. Gouda, dripping from the waist down,
crouches in front of him — no lecture, no diagram now, just a man who broke his own
rule and knows it.*

**GOUDA:** That's on me. I moved too fast because a number in a basin made me
hopeful, and hope's not a training plan. *(beat)* The reflex is real. What just
happened doesn't make it not real. It means knowing a thing and doing a thing live
in two different rooms, and I walked you into the second one before you'd finished
furnishing the first.

**KAI** *(hoarse, staring at the water)*: Friday. On the block. It — it wasn't like
this. It was quiet. This wasn't quiet.

**GOUDA** *(carefully, not lying, not explaining either)*: I know.

*He doesn't say anything else. He doesn't have an answer he's willing to give a
fifteen-year-old yet — or possibly at all. Kai doesn't push. Bo sits down on Kai's
other side, dripping, and doesn't say anything wise either — just stays.*

**INT. CLUBROOM — LATER, EMPTY EXCEPT MIREI**

*The private notebook, out again. She writes: **0:03 exposure, full relapse, resting
HR spike to 148.** Beneath it, the printed freediving benchmark table, the same one
from episode two — today's data point doesn't even belong on the same chart; it's
not an outlier above the curve anymore, it's off the axis entirely, in the other
direction.*

*She taps her pen, chewing her one long asymmetric strand of hair. Then, slowly,
writes a single sentence — the closest she's come yet to finishing the thought she
wouldn't finish in episode two — and we're close enough to read it this time:*

> *If the reflex is real and trained and his alone to control — Friday should have
> gone BETTER than today. Not worse. Something else was different that day.*

*She stares at it a long moment. Then closes the notebook without crossing it out
this time — a small, private escalation from "not yet" to something closer to
"not telling anyone yet."*

**MIREI** *(murmured)*: …Not him. Something *with* him.

*She doesn't like how that sentence sounds even inside her own head. She puts the
notebook away anyway.*

## ACT THREE (5:30)

**EXT/INT. FUJII FAMILY FISH STALL — HARBOR FRONT — EVENING**

*A warm, unglamorous little stall lit by a single bare bulb and a charcoal grill,
smoke curling into the dusk. BO'S MOTHER works the grill with the same unhurried
economy as Kai's grandmother works a stove — a family trait passed sideways across
two very different households. BO'S FATHER, the man from the cold open, sets out
mismatched stools without being asked. The whole club is folded in without ceremony:
Bo, Mirei primly perched with her clipboard set aside for once, Kai still quiet from
the afternoon.*

**BO'S FATHER** *(sliding a plate in front of Kai, no fuss)*: Eat. You look like a
man who went swimming with his clothes on.

**KAI** *(a small, surprised almost-laugh)*: …I didn't, actually.

**BO'S FATHER** *(shrugging, already moving on)*: Coach did, though. Word travels
fast on a harbor.

*Kai's hand goes, without him quite noticing, to the shell-cord bracelet at his
wrist — an old, grounding habit — as he eats. Nobody at this table asks him a single
question about the pool. Nobody performs sympathy at him either. It is, simply, a
table with room at it.*

**BO'S MOTHER** *(to Mirei, warm, direct)*: You're the one with the clipboard who
never eats enough. Sit closer to the grill.

**MIREI** *(caught off guard by being noticed rather than catalogued)*: I — yes.
Thank you.

*GOUDA arrives last, still in a dry change of clothes, hovering at the stall's edge
like a man unsure he's invited. Bo's father simply hands him a plate without a word,
settling it. Gouda sits, heavily, next to Kai — and for a moment looks like he might
say something about the pool again.*

**GOUDA** *(finally, low, just to Kai)*: I'm not going to apologize twice in one day.
It gets embarrassing for both of us.

**KAI** *(quiet, and meaning it more than the joke deserves)*: You don't have to
apologize at all. You pulled me out. That's the part that counts.

*Gouda doesn't have a response ready for that — a rare thing for him, same as it was
for Bo in episode two — so he just eats, and lets the moment be exactly as big as it
is without making it bigger. Around them: laughter, grease smoke, a transistor radio
somewhere playing something with an unhurried bassline nobody at this particular
table notices but Kai, who eats with it anyway, his body already fluent in a rhythm
none of these good people taught him.*

*A real, unforced peace — the second hearth scene the show has now earned, exactly
where episodes one and two's grandmother's-table beats trained the audience to
expect one, and exactly why moving it here, tonight, to a table Kai didn't grow up
at, lands as its own kind of found-family promise kept.*

**END CARD BEAT — INTERCUT**

**EXT. HARBOR ROAD — NIGHT**

*Kai walks home alone, full and warm from the stall, passing the dark chain-link of
the school pool on the way. He stops at the fence — not gripping it in fear this
time, just looking, hands loose in his pockets. He doesn't try the gate. He doesn't
need to tonight. He looks a moment longer, then keeps walking. Rest, for once,
instead of a wound.*

**INTERCUT — INT. A PRIVATE LAP POOL — SAME NIGHT**

*Somewhere colder and better-lit: an immaculate, empty training pool, House Kurose
banners folded and stacked rather than displayed — a place that doesn't need to
perform its own prestige. REN swims alone in the sense that no one is swimming
beside him — but at the pool's edge, silent, stopwatch in hand, an unspeaking HOUSE
ATTENDANT in Kurose blacks watches every length without once looking at a phone.
Even here, even for him, the Water's Rule holds: nobody swims unwatched.*

*Ren's turns are metronomic, flawless, contemptuous of effort. On one underwater
length, for a single unbroken beat, his eyes — ordinary silver-gray on land — go
cold Kuroshio silver beneath the surface, the water briefly, subtly *agreeing* with
him in a way no judge in a sanctioned meet would ever be allowed to see. He surfaces
at the wall. The silver is gone before he's finished blinking chlorine from his
lashes. The attendant clicks the stopwatch, notes a number, says nothing. Ren
towels off, unbothered, unwitnessed by anyone who'd know what they'd just seen.*

*Two boys, two pools, two kinds of alone — both, tonight, exactly as far from
unwatched as the Water's own rule requires. Hold on this a beat longer than feels
comfortable.*

**CUT TO BLACK. CREDITS.**

## POST-CREDITS (0:30)

**INT. LISTENING STATION — LOCATION UNKNOWN — NIGHT**

*The same cold room from episode one's post-credits. The TECHNICIAN, wool coat
still on, feeds a fresh spool of paper under the resonance drum's needle. The
waveform from fifteen years dormant is no longer a flat, occasional blip — it's a
steady, climbing pulse, denser than a week ago. She lays her ruler against it,
frowning, and measures twice.*

*Then she notices it: beneath the main waveform, so faint it could be instrument
noise, a second, thinner line tracing almost the same rhythm — out of phase by a
fraction, like an echo arriving before its source. She's never seen a harmonic do
that. She flips back through the logbook to the fifteen-year-old entry, holds the
two pages side by side under the lamp.*

**TECHNICIAN** *(to herself, very quietly)*: …That's not an echo. That's a second
signature.

*She doesn't pick up the phone this time. She just stares at the two lines a long
moment, unsettled in a way the first phone call never made her.*

**SMASH TO BLACK.**

## Writer's notes for draft 2 (things to decide on the redline)

1. **The basin-then-pool structure** — currently a two-step demonstration (safe
   basin success in Act One, real-pool relapse in Act Two) to dramatize "knowing a
   thing and doing a thing live in two different rooms." Option: cut the basin scene
   for pace and open Act Two directly on the pool attempt; costs the show its cleanest
   real-physiology beat (bible §5's promised centerpiece) but tightens runtime.
2. **Ren witnessing the relapse** — currently he's at the fence, clinically noting it
   in his own notebook and leaving wordlessly. Option: cut his presence from this
   scene entirely and hold his first real exposure to Kai's fear for episode 4's
   demonstration-fifty contempt beat instead, so his "so here's the shameful line's
   heir, drowning on purpose" reaction lands in one place rather than building twice.
3. **Ren's underwater eye-glow in the end card** — currently an early, wordless,
   almost-subliminal reveal of his awakened Current, a full episode ahead of his
   ep-4 demonstration fifty. Option: hold this reveal until episode 4 so the audience
   learns it at the same moment the school does, trading a rewatch-reward beat for a
   cleaner single point of revelation.
4. **Mirei's written hypothesis** — currently shown on the page in full ("something
   else was different that day... not him, something *with* him") rather than left
   blank the way her episode-two beat was. Option: withhold the actual sentence again
   and let only her murmured fragment carry it, per the season's stated preference for
   keeping her theory private until she's ready to act on it (beat sheet: "and says
   nothing, yet").
5. **Gouda wading in fully clothed** — currently played for a small, rueful human
   beat (his back, his soaked shoes) rather than heightened as a crisis. Option: slow
   it down further, cutting away to Bo's and Mirei's faces mid-rescue for an extra
   beat of real alarm before the relief, if the redline wants the relapse to land
   with more weight against episode one's mythic version of "Kai goes under."
