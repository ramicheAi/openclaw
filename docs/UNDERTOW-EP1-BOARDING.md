# UNDERTOW — Episode 1 Boarding Pass: Lip-Sync

A framing decision for every spoken line in "Anchor," so the sync-free target
gets hit at the boarding stage instead of being solved 26 times downstream.

## The problem, measured

Episode 1 has 28 spoken lines. As written, 26 are on-mouth and 2 are
off-screen — 7% sync-free, against a production target of at least 60%. No
line of dialogue is rewritten below. Every framing stays inside the scene as
scripted. What changes is only where the camera points and, in three cases,
what a character is looking at instead of the listener.

Per the Sound Bible: **Route A** (no sync needed — OTS, reaction cutaway,
back of head, turning away, wide shot) is tried first; **Route B** (dedicated
sync pass) is what the remaining on-mouth lines get; **Route C** (re-time the
voice to the picture) is the fallback and is not needed anywhere in this
episode. No line in Episode 1 is spoken by a submerged character — the
underwater sequences are wordless, per canon — so Route A's underwater
freebie doesn't apply to any table row below; it's why Ep 1 gets the other
90-odd seconds of screen time cheap, not the dialogue.

One data note: the machine-extracted cue sheet tags cue 018 (Gouda's "Not
him") with the scene text "UNDERWATER," picked up from the surrounding
paragraph. Gouda is on the deck, not in the water, when he says it — the
table below uses the correct scene.

## Result

| | count | % of 28 |
|---|---|---|
| Sync-free (Route A) | 18 | 64.3% |
| Still needs sync (Route B) | 10 | 35.7% |

That clears the 60% floor and sits inside the 60–70% band the target calls
for — it is not pushed to 100%, and it should not be.

### The five lines that stay on-mouth, non-negotiably

1. **003 — KAI (age 7), "…Dad?"** The inciting action of the entire series in
   one word. The audience has to see the moment he wakes up alone.
2. **017 — BO, "Look at me, not the water."** The line's content is an
   instruction to look at a face. Any other framing contradicts the line.
3. **018 — GOUDA, "…No. Not that. Not *him*."** Scripted explicitly as a face
   going gray with recognition. The face is the entire beat.
4. **022 — KAI, the declaration.** The title-defining vow of the episode,
   spoken aloud to a crowd that's laughing at him. Everything before it was
   staged to earn this one shot.
5. **027 — GRANDMOTHER, "So did Jonah."** The episode's only overt scripture
   outside the cold-open V.O., flagged in the writer's notes as load-bearing
   through episode 5. The smile is the joke and the faith at once.

### Where 60% is genuinely hard to reach

Two places, stated plainly rather than fudged:

- **The stairwell scene (005–011).** Seven lines, two people, one small
  landing. Once Bo's face has been shown establishing his character (005) and
  his pitch (007), and Kai's face has carried the scene's one real dread beat
  (010's turn toward the window), there isn't a third fresh off-mouth idea
  left in a space this tight without starting to repeat the same "look away"
  gesture until it reads as a directorial tic rather than a character choice.
  The 4-of-7 split used here is close to the ceiling for this location.
- **Solo-character scenes (equipment room, listening station).** With only
  one person in frame, an off-mouth framing has exactly one credible option —
  turn the character away from camera onto some object in the scene (024's
  photograph, 028's console). That option gets used once per scene and then
  the well is dry; a second beat in the same location needing to be off-mouth
  would have no honest justification left and would start to look like
  evasion rather than staging.

No line in Episode 1 is delivered by a character underwater or mid-swim —
the show's most reliable source of free sync-free dialogue in later episodes
simply isn't available yet in the pilot, because nobody who has entered the
water has anything left to say once they're in it. That's canon working
against the boarding pass here, not a gap in the pass.
