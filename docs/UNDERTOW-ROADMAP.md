# UNDERTOW — Production Roadmap: From Concept to Screen

Companion to [UNDERTOW-ANIME-BIBLE.md](UNDERTOW-ANIME-BIBLE.md) and
[UNDERTOW-ELEMENTS.md](UNDERTOW-ELEMENTS.md).

**The strategic truth this roadmap is built on:** anime series are almost never
greenlit from cold pitches. They are adapted from **proven IP** — a manga or webtoon
with a demonstrated audience, or a proof-of-concept that already went viral. Nobody
"sells an idea" to a studio; you build an asset the industry wants to license. So the
critical path is:

> **Lock the IP → build the pitch package → publish serialized proof → grow the
> audience → let the deal come to the traction.**

Every phase below has a **gate** — a concrete condition that must be true before the
next phase's spend makes sense.

---

## Phase 0 — Own It (weeks 1–2)

You cannot build on land you don't own. Before anything is shown publicly:

| Action | Detail | Rough cost |
|---|---|---|
| Canon lock v1 | Freeze the bible's four pillars (§14) + element registry as "Canon v1.0," dated. All future changes are versioned amendments. | $0 |
| Trademark search | Search "UNDERTOW" in animation/comics/games classes (USPTO TESS + WIPO). It's a common word — expect collisions; the JP title 逆流/Gyakuryū and a distinctive logo lockup are the fallback identity. Decide: keep, or pick a more ownable coined title. | $0 DIY / ~$500–1.5k with attorney |
| Copyright registration | Register the bible + registry as an unpublished literary work (US eCO). Cheap, fast, establishes the date. | ~$45–85 |

| IP hygiene | Keep canon docs in a private repo/vault once collaborators arrive; public repo is fine while it's solo. | $0 |

**Gate:** a dated, registered, locked Canon v1.0 owned by a defined entity. ✅→ Phase 1.

## Phase 1 — The Pitch Package (months 1–3)

The goal: a package a producer, artist, or investor can absorb in **15 minutes** and
repeat to someone else in **2**.

1. **Pilot teleplay** — Episode 1 "Anchor," ~22 min. *(First draft now exists:
   [UNDERTOW-EP1-SCRIPT.md](UNDERTOW-EP1-SCRIPT.md).)*
2. **Season 1 beat sheet** — 24 episodes, one paragraph each, from bible §8.
3. **Visual development** — character sheets (Kai, Ren, Bo, Mirei, Luna, Gouda), the
   three water palettes, five key-art pieces, ten House sigils. *(Briefs ready:
   [UNDERTOW-VISDEV-BRIEFS.md](UNDERTOW-VISDEV-BRIEFS.md) — usable as human-artist
   commissions or AI-generation prompts.)* Budget: ~$2–8k commissioned, or near-$0
   AI-assisted drafts to iterate taste before commissioning finals.
4. **Fathom rules card** — the power system on one illustrated page (bible §5 +
   elements §6). If it doesn't fit one page, it's too complicated for episode 7.
5. **Pitch deck** — 10–12 pages: logline, why-now, the five-anime engine map, world,
   cast, power system, season map, tone/comps, audience thesis (kids/adults table),
   the water-safety differentiator, and the ask.
6. **90-second teaser board** — the Episode 1 sinking sequence as boards/animatic.

**Gate:** package complete + three cold readers (not friends) can retell the premise
accurately after one pass. ✅→ Phase 2.

## Phase 2 — Proof of Concept (months 3–9) ← the critical phase

Pick the proof medium and start **shipping on a public cadence**. Recommended: both
lanes below in parallel, manga as the spine.

### Lane A — Manga / webtoon serialization (the spine)

- **Why:** it is the industry's native on-ramp. Shonen Jump+ / MANGA Plus Creators
  accept international submissions; WEBTOON Canvas → Originals is the western engine;
  self-published chapter drops (site + socials) keep full ownership. Solo Leveling
  itself walked the webtoon→anime road.
- **How:** partner with a mangaka/webtoon artist (revenue-share or page-rate,
  ~$50–150/page; a 40–50 page chapter 1 ≈ $2–7k). Chapter 1 = the Episode 1 script,
  re-broken for panels. Cadence target: a chapter every 3–4 weeks.
- **Adaptation note:** vertical-scroll webtoon format is *unfairly good* for this IP —
  depth is literally the scroll axis. Descending a Pillar in a webtoon is the medium
  advertising the show.

### Lane B — Animated teasers (the accelerant)

- 30–90 second pieces for TikTok/YouTube/Shorts/X: the sinking scene, a wake-dragon
  current-clash, an Ascended sighting. AI-assisted animation (Higgsfield et al.) makes
  this near-free to prototype; upgrade the best-performing concept with a small studio
  or freelance sakuga animator (~$3–15k/min) once one clearly hits.

### Both lanes require

- **Cultural consultants** — one per House region actually depicted early (start:
  Japan/ama tradition, Polynesia/wayfinding). Paid, credited. Non-negotiable for this
  IP's premise to be respect, not costume.
- **Sensitivity + theology read** — the Bible-teachings thread (elements §4) gets a
  pastoral/theological reader *and* an interfaith read, so reverence lands as written.

**Gate:** ≥6 published chapters and ≥3 teasers live, with a visible retention trend.
✅→ Phase 3 (which overlaps).

## Phase 3 — Audience (months 6–18, overlaps Phase 2)

Traction is the product now. The deal in Phase 4 is priced off these curves.

- **Channel strategy:** one home base (site/webtoon page) + three feeds (TikTok/Shorts
  for teasers, X/IG for art and lore drops, YouTube for compiled chapters/animatics).
- **Community:** Discord from chapter 1; the **power-scaling feed** is the engagement
  engine — canon feat-table drops, "who wins in shallow water?" debates, tier charts
  (elements §6 exists precisely to fuel this).
- **The unique lane:** water-safety partnerships. No battle anime has ever been able to
  call swim schools, lifesaving federations, and learn-to-swim charities natural
  partners. UNDERTOW can. This is PR, mission, and school-market distribution in one.
- **Benchmarks that make committees look** (order-of-magnitude, not law): webtoon
  50k+ subscribers or consistent top-canvas ranking; a teaser >1M organic views;
  Discord in the thousands with daily activity; merch/Patreon conversion signal.

**Gate:** at least two of the four benchmark families trending, sustained for a
quarter. ✅→ Phase 4.

## Phase 4 — The Deal (months 12–24)

Ranked routes; pursue in order, but traction may reorder them for you:

1. **Manga publisher → production committee (traditional engine).** If Lane A runs on
   a platform (Jump+/WEBTOON Originals), the platform itself shops hits to committees.
   Gives up: significant control and revenue share. Gets: the real machine — this is
   how almost every anime you love was made.
2. **Streamer original (Netflix / Crunchyroll).** Direct pitch armed with the package +
   traction data. Gives up: exclusivity, some upside. Gets: budget certainty, global
   day-one release.
3. **Indie / crowdfunded pilot.** Kickstarter a 5–12 min pilot (comparables have raised
   $100k–1M+); use the finished pilot as the ultimate pitch asset. Gives up: cash risk
   and years. Gets: maximum ownership and proof nobody can argue with.

**In every negotiation:** the canon docs are the moat. Creator consultation/approval
rights on bible-level canon are the hill to hold; episode-level details are the hill to
trade.

**Gate:** signed development/adaptation deal, or a funded pilot in production.

---

## Phase 5 — Production (the long game)

What a real 24-episode season means, so expectations are set now: a production
committee (publisher, streamer, studio, merch partners) financing ~$3–6M+; a studio
whose water/FX pipeline fits the show's hybrid 2D/FX ambition; 2–3 years from greenlight
to air. The creator's job in this phase is **canon guardian and taste court**: approve
designs against the visdev bible, protect the four pillars, and keep the surface-sport
reality and the safety ethic intact through every committee note.
