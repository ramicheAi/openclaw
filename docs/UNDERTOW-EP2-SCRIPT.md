## COLD OPEN (2:30)

**INT. EQUIPMENT ROOM — NIGHT — FRIDAY, HOURS AFTER THE POOL**

*The same footlocker from episode 1, still open. GOUDA, 44, sits on an overturned bucket
in the dark, the only light a bare bulb on a pull-string. The old photo again: young
Gouda, sharp and gold-medal-lean, arm around a second man in a dark second-skin suit,
both grinning at a camera in front of Old Build stonework neither of them is looking at
like tourists would.*

*Gouda's thumb rests over the second man's face. Doesn't move it. Doesn't need to.*

**SOUND, UNDER:** *distant, muffled — a stopwatch ticking, slow, like a heartbeat.
34. 33. 32.*

*A memory, not narrated, just shown: fast-cut, half a second each — a younger Gouda's
hand, underwater, going still on a lane rope. A judge's clipboard. A second man's hand
closing over Gouda's wrist and pulling, hard, up. Sunlight breaking the surface too late
to be relief.*

*Gouda blinks. He's back in the equipment room. His hand is shaking, very slightly. He
closes the locker — gently, this time, like closing a door on a sleeping kid — and sits
a moment longer in the dark.*

**GOUDA** *(to no one, barely audible)*: Land. Just land. That's all he gets from me.

*He says it like a rule he's setting for himself, not a decision about Kai at all.*

**TITLE CARD — EPISODE 2: DRY LAND**

## ACT ONE (6:00)

**INT. MUNAKATA HIGH SCHOOL — HALLWAY — MONDAY MORNING**

*The hallway is louder than usual, and Kai is the reason. A phone gets held up as he
passes — someone's re-watching Friday's video, tinny audio bleeding out: **"...swim to
the bottom of the sea..."** followed by canned laughter from whoever's watching it.*

**BOY #1** *(to his friend, not bothering to lower his voice)*: Anchor talks to the
ocean now. Did he answer back?

**BOY #2:** Four minutes, man. My uncle can't hold his breath for four *seconds*.

*Kai walks through it the way you walk through weather. His face gives away nothing —
but his jaw is different than it was on Friday. Not softer. Set.*

*BO catches up to him at a dead run, breathless, delighted, like Monday is Christmas.*

**BO:** They're saying it different today. Not just "Anchor sank." "Anchor talked to the
ocean and *lived*." That's basically a promotion.

**KAI** *(dry)*: Feels the same from in here.

**BO:** Give it time. Yesterday's the worst it's ever gonna be. Today it's just an
echo.

*Kai doesn't answer that. But he doesn't argue it either — which, from him, is
something.*

**EXT. POOL — PE CLASS — DAY**

*Full class, uniforms lined up on the deck. Kai's in borrowed jammers again — the school
never got him proper ones, on the theory he wouldn't need them twice. He stands at the
gutter's edge, toes an inch from the tile lip, water lapping close enough to wet his
foot.*

*He does not step down into it. His whole body has the specific stillness of someone
holding a door shut with their back.*

**PE TEACHER** *(checking a clipboard, not unkindly, just busy)*: Isozaki. In or out,
I've got thirty of you.

*Kai's hand finds the fence behind him without looking. GOUDA, watching from the
equipment shed doorway with a mug of instant coffee, sees it — the exact grip, white-
knuckled, that he saw Friday before the fall.*

*He crosses the deck before he's decided to.*

**GOUDA** *(to the PE teacher, easy, like it's nothing)*: He's with me today. Doctor's
note update. *(beat, to Kai, quieter)* Deck. Now.

*Kai steps back from the gutter like it let go of him first. Behind them, a couple of
students snicker — nothing cruel, just reflex.*

**INT. GYM — MOMENTS LATER**

*Gouda unrolls a floor mat. No water anywhere in sight — that's clearly the point.*

**GOUDA:** Land drills. Core, breath control, mobility. You don't touch the pool again
until I say so.

**KAI** *(guarded)*: Is this — are you actually coaching me now?

**GOUDA** *(too fast, too flat)*: I'm keeping you off a deck full of phones. That's all
this is.

*A beat. Kai studies him — the same look he gave the water Friday, like he's checking
what's underneath.*

**KAI:** You looked like you'd seen a ghost. On the deck. Friday.

**GOUDA** *(closing the subject, not unkindly)*: I looked like a man who needs his
coffee. Plank position. Go.

*Kai gets down on the mat. Gouda watches him hold it, counting silently on his fingers
— and his face, for just a second, is doing arithmetic that has nothing to do with core
strength. Then he catches himself and looks away first.*

*(Gouda's private hope, never said aloud: land is boring. Land is safe. Land is where
a kid like this quits.)*

**INT. CLUBROOM — AFTER SCHOOL**

*The four-person swim club, now officially four people. MIREI has a fresh sheet of
graph paper pinned beside the old noticeboard plea, ruled into a grid: a wall chart,
day one. Bo is delighted by its mere existence. Kai stands apart, arms crossed.*

**MIREI** *(pen tapping the chart, all business)*: Prefectural qualifying entry closes
end of term. Munakata needs four *swum* times logged per swimmer to field a team at
all — any stroke, any distance, doesn't matter how slow, they just have to be *swum*.

**BO:** We've got Kai's from Friday, though, right? That's one.

**MIREI** *(a beat; she chooses her words with more care than the moment seems to need)*:
Friday's time is logged. Whether it counts as "swum" is a question I have not asked the
federation office out loud, because I don't want to hear the answer yet.

**KAI** *(quiet)*: It doesn't count. I sank.

**MIREI:** You *finished*. There's a difference, on paper, if nobody looks too closely.
*(beat)* But that's one. We need four. And you don't currently swim.

*Silence. The math is ugly and everyone in the room can do it.*

**BO** *(the eternal optimist, but trying)*: So we get him swimming.

**MIREI** *(not unkind, just precise)*: He can't get his face in the water on purpose.
I watched him try during PE from the gallery. That's not a coaching problem I know how
to solve with a stopwatch.

*Kai says nothing. He looks at the chart — his own name, one lonely square filled in,
three empty ones stretching out after it like a countdown.*

## ACT TWO (6:30)

**EXT. HARBOR DOCK — DAWN, TUESDAY**

*Grey pre-sunrise light. BO, in his family's oilskins, hauls a net hand over hand,
breath fogging, singing the same tuneless happy song from episode one under his breath.
KAI sits cross-legged on a coil of rope, watching, uninvited but not asked to leave.*

**BO** *(not looking up from the net)*: You're up early for someone who hates water.

**KAI:** Couldn't sleep. *(beat)* Wanted to see how you do it. The breath thing.

*Bo drops the net, delighted, and drops down onto the dock across from him — all
business now, in the specific way only Bo can make "business" look like play.*

**BO:** Okay. This isn't magic, whatever Mirei's chart wants it to be. This is just what
my grandpa taught my dad and my dad taught me, because you can't haul net for six hours
if you're gasping every ten seconds. *(beat)* First thing — nobody does this alone. Not
even me. That's rule one before rule one.

**KAI:** ...Noted.

**BO:** I mean it. My dad's rule, not mine: you hold your breath with a buddy watching,
full stop, every time, forever. Watching means watching — not also holding *their*
breath at the same time as you, or who's gonna notice if you go weird?

*Bo demonstrates: three slow breaths in through the nose, a long, unhurried exhale
through pursed lips — no gasping, no hyperventilating, nothing dramatic.*

**BO** *(cont'd):* And never — never — big fast breaths before you hold. Feels like
it should help. It's how people black out on the way *up*, when it's too late to notice
anything's wrong. So: slow air in, slow air out, however many you need to feel settled,
then hold. Nice and boring.

**KAI** *(watching closely)*: That's it? That's the secret?

**BO** *(grinning)*: The secret is doing the boring thing every single day until it
stops being hard. C'mon. Try it. I'm right here.

*Kai closes his eyes — on the dock, dry, no water anywhere near his face — and breathes
the way Bo showed him. Holds. Bo counts on his fingers, watching Kai's face the whole
time, not his own stopwatch. Fifteen seconds. Twenty. Kai's hand twitches toward his own
throat — the old panic, small, testing him.*

**BO** *(soft, steady)*: You're on a dock. Nothing's coming for you. Breathe out when
you need to, not before.

*Kai breathes out at twenty-eight seconds — not great, not nothing. His shell-cord
bracelet has slid down over his knuckles from the effort; he pushes it back up his
wrist without thinking about it, an old habit older than any of this.*

**BO** *(genuinely delighted, like Kai just won something)*: Twenty-eight! That's a real
number. That's a start-of-the-ladder number.

**KAI** *(almost smiling, catching himself)*: You say that like it's good.

**BO:** It's *yours*. That's what's good about it.

**INT. KAI'S HOME — KITCHEN — MORNING, LATER THAT WEEK**

*Small, warm kitchen. GRANDMOTHER moves between stove and table with the unhurried
economy of someone who has cooked ten thousand breakfasts. Miso simmers on one burner;
on another, something spiced and pan-fried that isn't from any Japanese cookbook —
saltfish and dumpling, an old recipe she never grew up with and learned late, for
someone else's sake, and kept making after that someone was gone. A small jar of
fiery orange-red pepper sauce, hand-labeled in handwriting that isn't Grandmother's,
sits at the table's edge like it's always sat there. A transistor radio on the
windowsill plays low — not a J-pop station, something with an unhurried, rolling bass
line underneath it, the kind of rhythm you'd only notice if you were listening for it.
Grandmother hums along to it without seeming to notice she's doing it.*

*Kai eats fast, backpack already on one shoulder, the shell-cord bracelet catching the
window light as he reaches for his cup.*

**GRANDMOTHER** *(not looking up from the stove)*: You were at the harbor before the
sun. I heard the door.

**KAI** *(caught)*: Bo's teaching me to hold my breath. On land. It's not — it's not the
pool thing.

**GRANDMOTHER** *(a small, knowing sound, not quite a laugh)*: Mm. Everything starts on
land, child. Even the ark. *(beat, plainly, no weight put on it at all)* Eat the fish
part too. Not just the rice.

*Kai eats the fish part too. A beat of real, unforced peace — the version of this house
that has nothing to do with the sea trying to kill anyone.*

**KAI** *(quieter, almost to himself)*: Four times swum. By end of term. I've got one,
and it doesn't even count.

**GRANDMOTHER** *(finally looking at him, steady)*: Then you'd better start counting
the ones that will.

*She turns back to the stove before he can decide if that was comfort or a dare. Maybe
both.*

**INT. SCHOOL GYM — DAY**

*PE class, indoors today — mats, a mixed bag of dryland fitness stations. One station,
run by a bored student aide with a stopwatch, is a breath-hold contest: sit, hands on
knees, hold as long as you can, no funny business. It's meant to be a five-minute
time-filler. A small crowd gathers because it's more interesting than the balance-beam
station.*

*Most kids manage twenty, thirty seconds, laughing themselves out of it early. Then
Kai sits down.*

*He does Bo's three breaths — slow, visible, unhurried — which gets him one raised
eyebrow from the aide and nothing else. Then he holds.*

*Thirty seconds. The gym doesn't notice yet. Forty-five. A couple of heads turn.*

**BOY #2** *(from Act One, no longer smirking)*: ...Isn't that the anchor kid?

*One minute. Bo, watching from the mat line, is vibrating with the effort of not
shouting. Mirei, clipboard in hand for an unrelated reason, has gone very still, very
interested. Kai's face is calm — not the pool's eerie, unnatural calm, just a boy
concentrating, hard, on purpose.*

*One minute twenty. He breaks — controlled, unspectacular, exhaling in one long breath
and opening his eyes to a gym that is, for the first time in eight years, looking at
him with something other than pity or mockery.*

**STUDENT AIDE** *(checking the stopwatch, genuinely surprised)*: ...One twenty-two.
That's — that's actually really good, dude.

*No laughter. A couple of quiet "whoa"s. Someone claps once, uncertainly, and it
doesn't get shot down. Kai sits there a second, adjusting to the strange, unfamiliar
shape of a room not laughing at him.*

*Bo whoops loud enough for both of them. Kai's mouth does something that isn't quite a
smile yet, but is trying one on for size.*

**INT. CLUBROOM — LATER**

*Empty except for Mirei, alone, a spiral notebook open that is NOT the club's official
chart — smaller, private, dense with her own handwriting. Printed pages are taped in:
tables of published freediving benchmarks, static-apnea progressions for beginners,
neat column headers. Kai's numbers from the gym — 0:28, then later reps climbing
faster than the printed table says an untrained fifteen-year-old's numbers should
climb — are circled in red pen, twice.*

*She stares at the gap between his curve and the textbook curve for a long moment.
Then, carefully, she does not write anything more. Closes the notebook. Slides it into
the bottom of her bag, under her actual clipboard, where nobody — including,
implicitly, herself, for now — has to look at it again today.*

**MIREI** *(to herself, very quietly)*: ...Not yet.

**INT. FACULTY OFFICE — DAY**

*Gouda, feet on his desk as ever, is intercepted by the VICE PRINCIPAL with a folder.*

**VICE PRINCIPAL:** Prefectural registration. Four names, four times, by end of term,
or the pool's water-polo's problem again. *(beat, pointed)* I hear the Isozaki boy's
doing breathing exercises in your gym now.

**GOUDA** *(not looking up)*: Land drills. Nothing that gets anyone hurt.

**VICE PRINCIPAL:** That's not what I asked.

*Gouda's feet come down off the desk, slow, unhurried — a man buying himself a second
to choose his words.*

**GOUDA:** He's not swimming. I'm not training him to swim. I'm keeping him upright and
off a deck full of phones until he either finds his own way into that water or he
doesn't. *(quieter, mostly to himself)* Either way it's not going to be my hand that
puts him in it.

*He says it like a promise. His face says he already suspects it's a promise he won't
keep.*

## ACT THREE (5:30)

**EXT. HARBOR DOCK — LATE AFTERNOON, THAT WEEK — MONTAGE**

*Days compress: Bo and Kai on the dock, same rope-coil seats, same ritual — three slow
breaths, hold, Bo watching and counting on his fingers, never once looking at his own
chest. The numbers climb, each one a small, private, unfilmed victory: 0:45. 1:10.
1:40. Kai's face loses its flinch, rep by rep — not fearless, just familiar with the
discomfort now, the way you get familiar with cold water by the second toe you dip in,
not the first.*

*One rep, near the end of the montage, catches something real: as the seconds stretch
past two minutes, Kai's breathing before the hold falls, without him noticing, into the
same unhurried, syncopated rhythm as the radio's bassline from his grandmother's
kitchen — three in, hold, two out, like his body already knew a beat nobody taught him
in words.*

**BO** *(breaking the montage, checking his own battered wristwatch, stunned)*: Kai.
Kai — that's four minutes. That's a full four minutes, man, on a *dock*, on *purpose*.

*Kai opens his eyes. Doesn't gasp, doesn't scramble — just breathes out, slow, the way
he was taught. For a second he looks almost as unsettled by this number as he was by
Friday's.*

**KAI** *(quiet, working something out in real time)*: Friday. The pool. It was four
minutes too.

**BO:** ...Huh. Yeah. Guess it was.

**KAI** *(half to himself, and it costs him something to say)*: That one wasn't me. The
water did that one *to* me. *(beat, looking at his own hands like they belong to a
stranger he's getting to know)* This one's mine.

*Bo doesn't have a joke ready for that — a rare thing — so he just claps a hand on
Kai's shoulder, once, solid, and lets the moment be exactly as big as it is without
making it bigger.*

**EXT. SCHOOL POOL — DUSK, END OF THE WEEK**

*Empty pool deck, gates unlocked but the lights low, the pool itself a flat sheet of
darkening blue-gold. Kai walks out alone — or believes he is. In the shadow past the
equipment shed, unseen by Kai, GOUDA stands with his arms crossed, watching, having
followed at a careful distance the way he's followed every deck Kai's walked onto this
week without once announcing it.*

*Kai stops at the gutter — the same spot as Monday. Crouches. Presses two fingers
flat against the wet tile lip, right where the deck ends and the water starts, close
enough now that ripples touch his fingertips.*

*He does Bo's three breaths. Slow. In. Out. The ritual that worked, every time, on a
dock, in a gym, in front of a whole PE class.*

*He cannot make his foot cross the tile. Not an inch. Whatever's on the other side of
that line isn't the same problem breath control solved — and some deep, honest part of
him already knows it. He sits back on his heels, hand still resting on the gutter, not
retreating, not advancing. Just there. Held.*

*No trance. No stillness in his heart rate this time — nothing mythic in this frame at
all, which is its own kind of ache. Just a boy who got four minutes of one impossible
thing this week and still can't manage four inches of the other.*

*From the shadows, Gouda watches him not go in, and doesn't move to help and doesn't
leave either — the one kind of alone he's willing to let the boy be. His face is
unreadable in the low light, but his hand, at his side, has curled slowly into a fist
he doesn't seem to notice he's made.*

**END CARD BEAT — EXT. SCHOOL POOL — CONTINUOUS**

*Wide, still shot: Kai at the gutter, small against the darkening water; Gouda a
shadow by the shed, watching over him without announcing it, exactly as far from
"alone" as the Water's own unspoken rule requires. Neither speaks. Neither leaves
first. The pool takes no side. Hold on this a beat longer than feels comfortable.*

**CUT TO BLACK. CREDITS.**

## POST-CREDITS (0:30)

**INT. MIREI'S BEDROOM — NIGHT**

*Small, orderly desk lamp. Mirei, in pajamas, the private notebook open again —
unable to leave it alone after all, exactly as advertised. She's added a new page: a
hand-drawn curve plotting Kai's week against the printed textbook progression, his
line pulling further from the reference line with every entry instead of converging
toward it the way a normal outlier eventually should.*

*She taps her pen against the margin, chewing the one long asymmetric strand of hair
she always chews when calculating. Writes one line, small, then stops herself and
doesn't finish the thought on paper.*

**MIREI** *(murmured, to the empty room, glasses catching the lamp light)*: That's not
how bodies work.

*She looks at the circled numbers a moment longer. Closes the notebook. Doesn't put it
away this time — leaves it, deliberately, squared at the corner of her desk, like
something she's decided she isn't finished with after all.*

**SMASH TO BLACK.**

## Writer's notes for draft 2 (things to decide on the redline)

1. **The cold-open memory flash** (young Gouda's hand going still, a second man
   pulling him up) — currently four quick, wordless cuts with no dialogue. Option:
   cut it to a single frame, even more restrained, and let Gouda's line ("Land. Just
   land.") carry the whole weight alone.
2. **The breath-hold contest's crowd reaction** — currently warm and mostly kind.
   Option: keep one holdout mocking voice in the gym scene so the turn doesn't read as
   a total, too-easy reversal of episode 1's ending; a single unconvinced "so what,
   it's not swimming" line might sell the incompleteness of the win better.
3. **Kai's "this one's mine" line** — currently spoken aloud to Bo. Option: cut the
   line entirely and let the action (checking his own hands) carry it silently,
   per the show's stated preference for subtext over declaration.
4. **Grandmother's kitchen beat** — currently the episode's only scene carrying Kai's
   heritage, entirely through props and rhythm (the pepper sauce, the radio's bass
   line, the recipe) with zero dialogue naming any of it. Confirm this is enough
   presence for episode 2, or whether it's too brief against episode 1's fuller
   dinner-table scene — the intent is restraint, not absence.
5. **Mirei's unfinished sentence** in the post-credits — currently left blank on the
   page ("Writes one line... doesn't finish the thought"). Option: let the audience
   glimpse a fragment of what she almost writes, for a stronger visual hook; currently
   withheld on purpose to keep her hypothesis private until she's ready to act on it,
   per the beat sheet's "and says nothing, yet."
