## Three layers

**1. BREATH — everyone has it. Anyone can train it.**
Real freediving physiology, dramatized: the dive reflex, the still heart
(bradycardia), blood shift, CO₂ tolerance. Calm is the stat. Panic is the enemy.
*Bo's layer: the ladder work climbs without a drop of bloodline.*

**2. CURRENT — inherited. The dragon learns your name.**
The ocean's great currents are dragons. Eleven bloodlines resonate with them.
Three laws, never broken:
① Currents work **only in water**.
② Currents **amplify technique — never replace it** (a perfect stroke with a weak
Current beats a sloppy stroke with a strong one).
③ Two Currents in one lane **interfere** — the current-clash, the show's duel.

**3. FATHOM — the rank. How deep you can go and stay yourself.**

## The doctrine: *real at the surface, legend in the deep*

Spectacle scales with depth. School meets stay sport-true — powers hide inside legal
**Surface Forms** only fans learn to see. The deeper the venue, the bigger the anime:
named techniques (title-carded), **Sovereign Waters** (a swimmer declares their water —
two domains overlapping is war), **Lane Wars** (powers unrestricted in a 25m pool — a
knife fight in a phone booth), and Abyssal feats out of myth.

## The two ends of transcendence

Go past your limit **in fear, for yourself** → the deep takes you: **the Drowned.**
Be carried past it **in faith, for someone else** → **the Ascended** — the gold-lit
Watchers of the deep.
Same depth. Same water. The difference is why you went.

## The rules that keep it honest

- Every power-up shows its training bill on screen. No exceptions, ever.
- Tier gaps are hard: depth cannot be out-talented in deep water — but shallow,
  technical formats stay winnable, so the surface sport always matters.
- One feat-table break per season, maximum — and it's the season's biggest moment.
- The water never cares how strong you are. It asks one question: *can you stay
  yourself in it?*
