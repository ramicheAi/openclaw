## Arc 1 — "The Anchor" (eps 1–6)

### Episode 1 — "Anchor"

Eight years after seven-year-old Kai followed his father, open-water legend Daigo Isozaki,
into a black sea and came back without him, fifteen-year-old Kai is still the boy who sits
out swim class — until Bo Fujii's dying swim club needs a fourth body by Friday or loses the
pool to water polo. Bo's ask isn't about speed, it's about company, and it's the first crack
in eight years of Kai's isolation. Forced onto the block for a single logged length, Kai
panics and falls in gracelessly in front of a phone-camera crowd — and then, underwater, the
horror turns: his heartbeat drops to 34, the water gathers around him like it's listening, and
he sits on the pool floor calm for four impossible minutes while Coach Gouda goes pale for a
reason no one else understands. Hauled out and humiliated, Kai does not apologize to the
water — he declares war on it, saying his dream out loud for the first time: he will swim to
the bottom of the sea and bring his father home. The school laughs; a transfer student in a
Kurose-crest tracksuit does not. That night, a listening station registers a waveform that
hasn't appeared in fifteen years.

**Emotional core:** Kai's shame curdling into a declared, public dream.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the involuntary trance-dive (bradycardia response, not yet a
trained skill) — establishes the floor Kai starts from.
**Plant/payoff:** plants Ren's recognition, the hydrophone signal, and Gouda's hidden past
with Daigo; the declared dream is the promise the whole season re-earns.

### Episode 2 — "Dry Land"

Monday brings a school's worth of "Anchor talks to the ocean now" jokes, but the four minutes
changed nothing about the thing that actually matters: Kai still cannot make himself put his
face in water on purpose. Gouda, having recognized in Kai's stillness something he buried
years ago, refuses to be the one who trains him and instead assigns him land-only drills,
privately hoping the boy quits before he has to decide anything. Bo, delighted to have a
training partner, teaches Kai the breath-hold ladder he learned hauling nets at dawn, and for
once Kai does something with his body in public that isn't a public failure. Mirei, unable to
leave an anomaly alone, starts a private notebook logging Kai's numbers against published
freediving charts and finds he's off every one of them — and says nothing, yet. The season's
ticking clock arrives on the noticeboard: Prefectural qualifying entry requires four *swum*
times on the books, and Kai has only ever sunk one. Kai ends the episode able to hold his
breath four minutes on the dock and still unable to put a toe past the pool's gutter.

**Emotional core:** Gouda's guilt-driven avoidance versus Kai's first, tiny, land-bound
competence.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** static apnea / purge-breath training (Breath layer, real
freediving method) as the literal floor of the power system.
**Plant/payoff:** plants Mirei's secret notebook and the Prefectural entry clock; pays off
episode 1's dream by pricing what living up to it actually costs.

### Episode 3 — "Still Heart"

Because Kai can hold his breath on land but still seizes at the water's edge, Gouda finally
gives the club the dive-reflex lecture he's been avoiding — cold water on the face, heart rate
dropping, the body remembering it was once a sea creature — reframing Kai's fear as physiology,
not cowardice. But understanding a door isn't opening it: Kai's first attempt to submerge his
face on command collapses into a full public panic relapse, and Gouda has to pull him bodily
from the shallow end, proof that knowledge without repetition is nothing. Bo's friendship holds
the floor where the training fails — that evening the whole club eats dinner at his family's
fish stall, warm and unhurried, a hearth scene the show earns before it asks for anything
harder. Mirei's notebook gains a hypothesis she isn't ready to say out loud: that Kai's
heart-rate crash in episode 1 wasn't him mastering panic at all. Ren, observed only in
passing, transfers formally into Kai's year, unremarked but noted.

**Emotional core:** the gap between knowing and doing, and Bo's friendship absorbing the
failure.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the mammalian dive reflex and bradycardia, taught explicitly as
real physiology before any mythic layer touches it.
**Plant/payoff:** domestic beat (harbor/fish-stall table); plants Mirei's unspoken hypothesis
and Ren's arrival, both paid off later this arc and next.

### Episode 4 — "The Line at the Bottom"

After the relapse, Gouda builds Kai a real incremental-exposure ladder — three seconds face
down, then five, then eight — unglamorous, slow, and logged on a wall chart Mirei starts
keeping in the clubroom. Ren, now visibly enrolled and openly gifted, swims a demonstration
fifty that leaves the whole school gaping, then watches Kai's small, humiliating drills with
open contempt: House Kurose raised him on the story that the Isozaki current was the Houses'
greatest shame, and here is its heir, drowning by inches on purpose. Stung rather than
crushed, Kai pushes past his trained interval on his own and — for the first time — opens his
eyes underwater without his chest locking, a small, private, entirely earned win nobody
films. Bo notices before anyone says anything, and doesn't make it a big deal, which is
exactly why it lands. Mirei's chart gets its first real upward tick.

**Emotional core:** Kai's first private victory landing right next to Ren's contempt.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new (consolidation of the breath-ladder method already
established).
**Plant/payoff:** plants the recurring clubroom wall chart and Ren's contempt-curiosity, both
paid off across the series; pays off episode 3's failed attempt.

### Episode 5 — "Marlin"

A tri-school time trial is announced — Munakata's one shot at a real result before Prefectural
qualifying opens — and Ren, guesting at Munakata for a cross-school exchange, needles Gouda in
front of the club about "coaching a drowned line," rattling Gouda more than Ren knows he can.
Humiliated in front of Bo and Mirei, Kai doesn't take the bait; he asks Bo to train him harder
instead, a small but real inversion of the hot-headed shonen instinct — pride loses to
usefulness. That night at Kai's grandmother's table, she tells the Noah story properly for the
first time in the show, unhurried, matter-of-fact, no sermon — a scene the show will ask the
audience to remember in five episodes' time. Mirei quietly reclassifies her private notebook
from curiosity to concern once she runs Kai's newest numbers. The tri-school meet is confirmed
for next week, with Kai entered in the one heat nobody else wanted.

**Emotional core:** Kai choosing humility over pride in the face of Ren's cruelty.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new.
**Plant/payoff:** domestic beat (grandmother's table, the Noah story) — the deliberate plant
this beat sheet pays off in episode 10; pays off episode 4's rivalry seed.

### Episode 6 — "First Length"

At the tri-school meet Munakata barely fields entries, and Kai draws the courtesy lane nobody
wants: last-place fifty free in a near-empty heat. His dive is still bad and his stroke is
still worse, but when the old panic reaches for him on the turn he doesn't sink this time — he
uses the breath ladder Gouda and Bo built and keeps moving, finishing dead last by twenty
seconds, on his own power, at the wall. Ren, spectating for his own school, watches the finish
with something that isn't contempt anymore, and says nothing. The mostly-empty stands don't
care, but Bo, Mirei, and Gouda react on the deck like Kai won gold — a last-place finish
staged as the arc's earned emotional peak, built to work with the sound off. That night Gouda
finally opens his old footlocker photo again and decides, for the first time, to actually
coach Kai rather than protect him from the water. Unnoticed by the crew, a stranger in a
federation blazer watches Kai's number from the stands and writes something down.

**Emotional core:** the club's unfiltered joy at a last-place finish — Arc 1's sound-off peak.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new (payoff episode, not an introduction).
**Plant/payoff:** pays off episodes 1–5's slow breath-ladder work with Kai's first finished
race; plants the Meridian scout who opens Arc 2.

## Arc 2 — "The Hidden Lane" (eps 7–12)

### Episode 7 — "Night Meet"

The scout from episode 6 pays Gouda a formal visit, blazer and all, talking about a "youth
development camp" — recruitment language Gouda recognizes and can't explain without exposing
his own past. Meanwhile a new face at the harbor, Tsukiko "Luna" Amane, an ama-diver-island
free-diver who treats the pool as a formality, watches Kai breathe on the dock and clocks
exactly what he is — then appoints herself his teacher without asking permission, correcting
years of Bo's fisherman-taught habits with real technique inside one afternoon. House Kurose
tasks Ren with quietly confirming Kai's identity, a job he takes with visible unease rather
than eagerness. That night Luna presses an invitation into Kai's hand: a "night meet," no
federation letterhead, no explanation. Kai stands at his window with it long after dark,
torn between the fear that built his whole life and a curiosity bigger than it.

**Emotional core:** Kai's temptation toward the deep world versus his built-in fear of it.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** proper freediving equalization and cold-water bradycardia
conditioning, taught by Luna as a real leap beyond Bo's dock methods.
**Plant/payoff:** plants the night-meet invitation and Ren's assignment; pays off episode 6's
scout glimpse.

### Episode 8 — "What the Blazer Wants"

Against Gouda's explicit rule to leave "the deep stuff" alone, Kai sneaks out with Luna to the
night meet — his first real act of defiance, not of Gouda's authority so much as his own fear.
The address turns out to be a sealed harbor door that only opens to a controlled exhale at an
exact pitch, and getting through it costs Kai hours of failed attempts and a bloodied lip
before Luna's drilling finally pays off — the season's first real training bill for something
mythic rather than physical. A quick, quiet scene at home has Kai lying to his grandmother
about where he's going, a small domestic beat made tense rather than warm, the cost of the
secret already showing. Ren, tailing Kai on House orders, nearly gets caught and instead
glimpses Luna, whom his own family keeps files on — deepening his suspicion that this is
bigger than one anomalous swimmer. The seawall door finally opens on Kai's own breath, and the
water swallows him and Luna whole while Ren watches from the dark.

**Emotional core:** Kai's first deliberate choice to go toward the fear instead of away from
it.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the breath-pitch lock — Old Build resonance technology, the
season's first mythic-tech reveal.
**Plant/payoff:** domestic beat (tense, not warm — the lie to grandmother); plants Ren's
active surveillance, paid off in episode 9.

### Episode 9 — "The Drowned Lane"

Past the gate, Kai, Luna, and a talked-into-it, protective Bo descend together into a
bioluminescent, drowned Nagi ruin — bark-glyphs the size of buildings, an Old Build branch-hall,
marine snow falling upward in the current — the show's biggest set piece yet, and a buddy-dive
by mechanical necessity, not sentiment. A real physiological wall hits Kai at a depth past
anything he's trained for, and it's Luna's buddy-rescue technique, not Kai's own gift, that
gets him back to a safe pocket — the season's clearest proof yet that Currents amplify
discipline, they don't replace it. Steadied, Kai has his first conscious, deliberate taste of
the Undertow answering him on purpose — a small, controlled current-read, nothing like the
accidental trance of episode 1. They surface having proven the hidden world is real and vast,
not myth — the "the pool was the walls" turn the whole season has been building to. Breaking
the surface, they find a ring of masked Meridian juniors already waiting in torchlight,
second-skin suits under formal blazers: the deep world hasn't just been found — it's been
watching.

**Emotional core:** the terror and awe of proof — the world just got enormous.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the first voluntary, controlled use of Kai's Current (a
flow-line read at depth), earned by every episode since the pilot and paid for in real
jeopardy; buddy-rescue technique as the thing that actually saves him.
**Plant/payoff:** pays off episode 1's accidental trance by contrast with earned control;
plants the masked Meridian juniors, paid off next episode.

### Episode 10 — "Two Shores"

Surfacing into the Meridian's ring forces the reveal proper: Luna and a senior officiant lay
out Nagi, the First Drowning, the ten Houses, and the Fathom ladder for Kai and Bo — careful,
incomplete, but real. Reeling, Kai goes home that night and hears his grandmother's Noah story
land differently in hindsight than it did in episode 5 — faith and Meridian history describing
one flood from two shores, quietly, without either side needing to win the argument. Bo,
frightened for his friend in a way his usual cheer can't cover, has the season's first honest
crack in the found-family floor: doubt that this world is safe for Kai at all. Left out of the
dive, Mirei notices Kai and Bo hiding something and starts her own quiet file, planting her
eventual read into the deep world. Gouda finds out — from Luna, not from Kai — that the boy
he's been failing to protect has already been to a ruin, and the anger that follows is aimed
entirely at himself.

**Emotional core:** Kai reframing his grandmother's faith through the world's true history.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the Nagi/First Drowning history, formally delivered in-world for
the first time.
**Plant/payoff:** domestic beat (grandmother's Noah story, recontextualized); pays off episode
5 in full; plants Mirei's exclusion and Gouda's guilt, both paid off next episode.

### Episode 11 — "Marlin's House"

Cornered by the Meridian's sudden interest, Gouda finally tells Kai a partial truth: Daigo
wasn't just a drowned swimmer, he was a Warden — a fact that recasts the dream Kai declared in
episode 1 as something far heavier than he knew. The same day, House Kurose confirms to Ren
directly that Kai carries the Isozaki Undertow, the "shameful dead current" his whole
upbringing was measured against, and Ren doesn't process it — he confronts Kai, half jealous,
half grieving for a life spent chasing a gift Kai has by birth and can't even use. Stung and
reckless with new information, Kai accepts Ren's dare to race him in open water at night, well
past anything he's actually trained for, and has a genuine, dangerous panic relapse in front of
witnesses — several episodes of hard-won control undone in one bad decision made for the worst
reason. Bo pulls him out; Ren walks away first, unable to look at what he's done, and the
friendship barely begun between them is now openly adversarial. Kai ends the episode alone on
the dock, humiliated in a new way — this time by someone he'd quietly started wanting respect
from.

**Emotional core:** Ren's grief-shaped cruelty meeting Kai's need to be taken seriously — and
both losing.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none (a regression episode by design — proof that gains can be
lost to a bad choice, not just failed to be earned).
**Plant/payoff:** pays off Ren's contempt-curiosity from episode 4 and the ep-1 Ren-watching
plant; plants the Kurose "shallow current" wound, paid off across Arc 3 and into Season 2.

### Episode 12 — "Low Tide"

Wanting to quit the deep world entirely, Kai finds Gouda — guilt-heavy enough to almost let
him — until the crew holds the line episode 1 promised: Bo repeats his founding thesis, you
don't have to swim fast, just swim with us; Mirei finally shows Kai her notebook, reframing his
worth as a fact to train honestly rather than a fluke to be shielded from; Luna admits she has
her own reasons for staying at the Meridian's edge instead of inside it. Alone, Ren visits his
family's training hall and we see, for the first time, the cost behind his cruelty: his Current
awakened shallow, and House Kurose has never forgiven shallow, recasting episode 11 as fear
wearing contempt's face. Gouda agrees to train Kai in earnest — deep world included — on one
condition: he'll teach him exactly how far not to go. The scout returns with a formal, final
summons: entry to the Twilight qualifiers is open, and Kai is invited to try. In the clubroom,
Mirei's wall chart gets a new column, quietly labeled "Fathom."

**Emotional core:** the crew's collective loyalty overriding Gouda's fear, and Ren's wound
humanized without being excused.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new — the Fathom chart formalized as the season's visible
progression ledger.
**Plant/payoff:** pays off Bo's episode-1 line and Mirei's secret notebook; sets up Arc 3's
qualifiers; plants Ren's shallow-current wound for later payoff.

## Arc 3 — "Twilight Trials" (eps 13–19)

### Episode 13 — "Breath Gates"

With the qualifiers now a real date, Gouda formalizes Kai's training into named "breath
gates" — sequential, measurable thresholds of static apnea, CO₂ tolerance, and recovery — each
one logged on the wall chart, each one slow. The surface world doesn't pause for this: the
tri-school result has provisionally qualified Munakata for Prefectural preliminaries, and
Mirei, now captain in practice if not title, has to run two calendars at once without ever
letting the visible one slip — her own quiet arc of competence starting here. Luna arrives to
supervise the deep-specific gates Gouda can teach but can no longer verify himself, and the
two adults' guarded professional respect hints at history neither explains. Denied a Current
his whole life, Bo throws himself at the same gates with monk-like discipline, his numbers
climbing at a rate that owes nothing to bloodline. The episode ends on Kai clearing his first
breath gate — unglamorous, just a stopwatch and a nod — establishing the training-bill promise
before any race happens.

**Emotional core:** the dignity of slow, unglamorous, measurable work.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the breath-gate system, formally named — the training-arc
structure the rest of Arc 3 is built on.
**Plant/payoff:** plants Bo's parallel Breath-only progression, paid off in episode 19.

### Episode 14 — "Two Calendars"

The Prefectural preliminary heats arrive and demand real stakes with zero mythic help; Kai,
Bo, and a returning Mirei — pulled back into the lane, exactly as she feared and hoped — have
to perform in front of an ordinary crowd that has no idea what any of them are also training
for underwater. The night before, the crew eats at Kai's grandmother's table, her blessing over
the meal quietly covering both of Kai's lives at once. In the water, Kai posts his best
legitimate surface time yet — still slow, but clean and present, no panic — a small,
unglamorous payoff of months of breath-ladder work. That night Luna finally teaches the crew
the three laws of Currents properly — water-only, technique first, interference — staging a
harmless demonstration clash between herself and Gouda in the empty school pool so Kai can see
two flows argue over one lane before he ever has to survive it himself.

**Emotional core:** the surface world's ordinary stakes made to matter as much as anything
underwater.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the three laws of Currents, taught in a safe demonstration (not
yet competitive).
**Plant/payoff:** domestic beat (grandmother's table); pays off episode 13's gate training with
a real surface result; sets up episode 17's competitive payoff.

### Episode 15 — "Undertow, Named"

In a supervised practice tank, Kai's first real attempt at a current-pull goes further than
planned: instead of moving him through the water, it tugs at the water itself, unsettling even
Luna and draining the color from Gouda's face in a way the audience now understands. The
Meridian's qualifier bracket is announced — five events (distance, technical, retrieval,
buddy-rescue, open-water) — and the confirmed roster leaks names that rattle the crew: House
champions Nereus Thalassa and Aput Sedna, and, unexpectedly, a wayfinder said never to lose by
force, Moana-Rangi Tangaroa. Still raw from episode 11, Ren asks to spectate rather than
compete this cycle, quietly working through what his shallow Current means with elders who've
run out of patience — a thread the show deliberately leaves open. Kai sees his own name on the
official roster for the first time, in ink, no longer a rumor the deep world is chasing but a
competitor it has to answer to.

**Emotional core:** Kai's dawning unease that his gift might be stranger and larger than
anyone, including him, understands.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new (a plant episode); the qualifier format is laid out in
full for the audience.
**Plant/payoff:** plants the Undertow's deep-affecting nature (paid off much later in canon)
and Moana-Rangi's reputation, paid off in episode 18.

### Episode 16 — "Retrieval"

The Twilight qualifiers open in a Pillar branch-hall arena; event one is relic retrieval, a
timed dive where the relic's weight mechanically enforces the buddy system, and Kai is paired
at random with a stranger from a rival House, forced to trust an unfamiliar swimmer faster
than he's ever trusted anyone. His pair clears the event only barely, saved less by his
Current than by remembering Luna's buddy-rescue drills to the letter — discipline outranking
bloodline again, on the record this time. Bo, paired separately, posts a clean, unspectacular
clear that quietly outperforms several bloodline swimmers — the first hard data point toward
his Breath-only qualification. Nereus Thalassa is glimpsed for the first time, entering the
water like he's already decided everyone else will panic before the start — a philosophy of
swimming stated before a word of dialogue. The night before the standings post, the crew shares
a quiet meal together in temporary lodging near the venue, and the standings arrive the next
morning with Kai barely inside the cut line and Bo comfortably above it.

**Emotional core:** the fear of being the weak link in a system that punishes solo mistakes.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the buddy-rescue/relic-retrieval event format — the arc's one new
mechanic for this competition episode.
**Plant/payoff:** domestic beat (a shared meal before the venue); pays off episode 9's
buddy-rescue lesson under real competitive stakes.

### Episode 17 — "Current-Clash"

Event two is a straight lane race against a drawn opponent with an awakened Current, and Kai
draws a Sedna-trained swimmer whose cold-water stillness nearly beats him on composure alone —
Kai's first real, competitive current-clash, animated as interfering wake-patterns and decided
by the smallest technical margin. He nearly panics at the clash's disorientation midrace and
claws the win back not with anything mythic but with Bo's dock-taught breath ladder — the
season's required mid-race reversal, won by training, not talent. Mirei, watching from the
gallery, calls the race better than the official commentary, a small beat that plants her
future beyond "manager." Ren watches from the stands, visibly undone by how far Kai has come
without him, and the show sits in his jealousy without resolving it. The standings show Kai has
technically already qualified for the finals on points, before the marquee race everyone came
to see even starts.

**Emotional core:** the terror of losing composure mid-fight, and clawing it back on drilled
instinct rather than gift.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** the competitive current-clash, formally paid off from episode
14's demonstration — the arc's one new mechanic for this competition episode.
**Plant/payoff:** pays off the three laws taught in episode 14; plants Mirei's tactical eye,
paid off in Season 2.

### Episode 18 — "Wayfinder"

The qualifiers' open-water leg pairs Kai against Moana-Rangi Tangaroa, who never swims the
shortest line — she reads the whole current field and takes the long way that turns out to be
the fast way — and for the first time all season, sheer will and a good heart aren't enough:
Kai loses clean, decisively, with no dirty trick and no coincidence to blame. He doesn't sink,
doesn't panic, doesn't embarrass himself — he is simply, honestly outclassed, and the show lets
the loss carry the same weight a win would. In a separate event, Bo delivers the season's other
big number: a qualifying time on the combined distance and buddy-rescue legs, on Breath alone,
loud enough that House scouts in the stands are visibly recalculating what they thought was
possible. Instead of gloating, Moana-Rangi offers Kai a hand up out of the water and one
sentence that reframes his entire race. Ren, still spectating, watches the loss land on Kai
without cruelty for the first time all arc.

**Emotional core:** the dignity of an honest loss to a better swimmer.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new (the arc's marquee race, run entirely on established
mechanics).
**Plant/payoff:** pays off Moana-Rangi's episode-15 reputation with the promised "loss that
matters"; plants her lesson, paid off next episode.

### Episode 19 — "The Long Way"

Moana-Rangi spends an off-hours session teaching Kai not a new trick but a new question: he's
been racing the water like an enemy to survive it, when Tangaroa practice is reading it like a
friend giving directions — reframing the Undertow's downward pull as something he could listen
to, not just endure, a lesson that costs him nothing physically and everything he thought he
knew about his own gift. Bo's Breath-only qualification is made official, and Gouda — who spent
the whole arc insisting bloodline isn't destiny — finally says so to Bo's face, the bible's
founding thesis paid off in full. The whole crew, including a still-prickly Ren invited by Bo
and a still-guarded Luna, share a harbor meal to mark it, arc-ending warmth before the season's
final storm. Mirei's wall chart now shows Kai's and Bo's names climbing on entirely different
axes — work versus bloodline resolved as two honest paths, not a contest. The finals bracket
posts: Kai and, remarkably, Bo have both qualified through — and a bureaucratic-sounding notice
arrives that the finals will open, for the first time in Meridian history, to surface press
credentials.

**Emotional core:** Bo's Breath-only qualification landing as the arc's earned, sound-off-ready
peak.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new (a lesson-and-consolidation finale for the arc).
**Plant/payoff:** domestic beat (harbor meal); pays off episode 18's loss and thirteen episodes
of Bo's parallel training; plants the surface-press credentials that Arc 4 detonates.

## Arc 4 — "The Second Tide" (eps 20–24)

### Episode 20 — "The Second Tide"

Before the finals begin, the show finally puts a face to the name whispered since episode 15:
Kaien Nakaru, former Fathom Games champion of House Ryn, whose hometown drowned in a tsunami
the Meridian's deep-listeners saw coming forty minutes early and said nothing about — the
audience meets him as a tragedy first, a philosophy second, a threat third. At the venue,
surface press badges sit beside Meridian robes for the first time in the show's history, and
Ren, drafted by his House to "manage optics" for the cameras, is handed the job of protecting
the very secrecy that broke his rival's family. Gouda recognizes a face in the crowd from his
own championship years and goes cold well before anyone else notices anything is wrong. Kai and
the crew arrive buzzing, oblivious, thrilled just to be there. The finals' opening ceremony
begins, gorgeous and enormous, exactly as Nakaru's people finish taking their positions unseen.

**Emotional core:** dread building under celebration — the crew's joy at exactly the moment
it's least safe.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new (villain-introduction episode).
**Plant/payoff:** pays off episode 19's press-credential detail; plants Nakaru's full
introduction ahead of the strike.

### Episode 21 — "Unmasking"

Mid-event, the Second Tide executes its plan — not murder, an unmasking: killing the venue's
Old Build wards just long enough for surface cameras and civilians to see the drowned arena and
the Houses' regalia, undeniable, unerasable, forcing the Meridian's cover-up machinery into
ugly, visible action right in front of Kai. Mid-race, Kai has to choose between finishing —
his hard-won surface promise — and pulling trapped bystanders clear using everything the
qualifiers just taught him; he chooses the rescue, losing the race and proving, in action, the
thesis twenty episodes have built. Ordered by his House to help suppress the leak, Ren instead
breaks ranks for the first time to help Kai clear people out — the relationship the season has
been building finally turns a corner it doesn't turn back from. Bo's race is lost too, swallowed
in the same chaos, and nobody in the crew cares. Across the wrecked venue, Nakaru is visible to
Kai for one long moment — not gloating, just watching — before he vanishes into the water.

**Emotional core:** Kai choosing a stranger's safety over his own dream, on instinct, without
being asked to.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new — an established-mechanics chaos episode, deliberately
not a clean new toy.
**Plant/payoff:** pays off the buddy-rescue training from episodes 9 and 16; pays off Ren's
arc-long ambivalence with a permanent turn.

### Episode 22 — "What the Silence Cost"

With the surface world's attention now impossible to manage, Gouda and Luna finally tell Kai
the whole truth of Nakaru: the Meridian's deep-listeners detected his hometown's tsunami forty
minutes early and said nothing, honoring the oath that the surface must not know, and his
family died in that documented, undeniable silence. Fresh off choosing a stranger's safety over
his own race, Kai can't make the Meridian's math work anymore, and the episode refuses to let
anyone excuse the institution for him. Now that secrecy has stopped being possible, Mirei turns
her data-mind on the Meridian itself and starts asking the questions the Houses have spent
twelve thousand years making sure nobody asks. At home, Kai's grandmother, hearing the news
alongside everyone else, tells him plainly that the sea doesn't forgive men for what they let
happen in its name — indicting the Houses without indicting the water. Cornered, the Meridian
announces an emergency tribunal — and quietly orders the sealed Warden archive pulled for
review.

**Emotional core:** Kai's institutional trust breaking cleanly, with nowhere soft to land.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new (the arc's central moral reveal).
**Plant/payoff:** domestic beat (grandmother's table); pays off Nakaru's introduction in full;
plants the Warden archive, opened next episode.

### Episode 23 — "The Ledger"

Forced to produce a paper trail to survive the surface world's scrutiny, the Meridian opens its
sealed Warden archive for the first time in years, and Gouda — summoned to testify to what he
witnessed the day Daigo "drowned" — takes Kai with him against explicit orders, deciding that
hiding this one truth is the same crime the Meridian just got caught committing. Ren, having
already broken with his House once, provides the access his family name still buys — the
clearest proof yet the mirror relationship has permanently turned. They don't find what they
came for; by the kind of accident that creates a problem rather than solving one, they find a
Warden's ledger bearing a signature dated three years after Daigo Isozaki's recorded drowning.
Nobody in the room can explain it, least of all Gouda, whose face does more work than any line
of dialogue could. The tribunal's session starts without them, the ledger unaccounted for, and
Kai holds the one piece of evidence that turns his father from a grief into a question.

**Emotional core:** Gouda choosing honesty over protection, at real professional risk to
himself.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new (a lore-reveal episode by design).
**Plant/payoff:** coincidence creates the problem, per craft rule — the ledger's existence pays
off nothing, it opens the finale's central mystery.

### Episode 24 — "Deep Calls to Deep"

Cornered by public pressure and its own rot, the tribunal votes to expose only what it must —
Nakaru's town, the silence, an incomplete reckoning — and quietly buries the ledger and
everything it implies, the same institutional reflex Nakaru has spent eleven years fighting; Kai
has to decide, for the first time as someone the world no longer gets to write off, what he does
with a truth the adults around him would rather forget. The season answers its opening question
cleanly and quietly: at the pool at dawn — no race, no trance, no crowd, only Gouda, who
unlocked the gate and stands silent on the deck, because the one kind of alone he permits Kai
now is watched-over alone — Kai dives face-first on his own choice and swims one full length
under his own power: competent, unremarkable, entirely his, exactly as slowly earned as the
breath-gate ladder promised.
The crew closes the season at the harbor — Bo's Breath-only qualification and Kai's Twilight
standing framed side by side on the clubroom wall, next to the circled spring date of the
Prefectural finals their prelim result earned: both calendars, still running, on one wall. Ren
is present and at ease among them for the first time. In the last minutes, Gouda shows Kai the ledger privately, and Grandmother finally
shares a memory of the night Daigo went under that she's withheld the whole season; memory and
ledger sit side by side with no answer given. The camera makes its long, patient descent from
the harbor down to the sealed root-lattice one more time, the same low tone from episode one now
sounding less like a note and more like a word.

**Emotional core:** the quiet, private triumph of a calm, unwatched length of pool — earned, not
declared.
**Mechanic/lore introduced:** none new — the finale deliberately spends existing mechanics
rather than adding one.
**Plant/payoff:** answers Arc 1's opening question (yes, Kai can swim) in action, not speech;
pays off the ledger and Grandmother's withheld memory into the season's closing double
question — is Daigo alive, and what is the Meridian still hiding.
