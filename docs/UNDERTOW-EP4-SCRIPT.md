## COLD OPEN (2:30)

**INT. A HOUSE KUROSE HALL — LACQUER CORRIDOR — SEVEN YEARS AGO**

*Not a training hall. A corridor — long, dim, immaculate, smelling of cedar and cold
stone. Along one wall, ranked like a library of oars: lacquered NAME-BOARDS, black,
each cut with one family name in silver. Dozens of them. Generations. A BOY of nine
walks it with his hands at his sides because he has been told to — REN, before the
fade, before the posture became armor, already the same face. Ahead of him a WOMAN
moves at a pace he has to work to match; we see her from the shoulders down only, a
plain dark sleeve, a cloth in one hand. She is not introduced. She will not be, this
season.*

**WOMAN** *(not slowing)*: Read them.

**REN (age 9)** *(reciting as he walks)*: Kurose. Thalassa. Yemoja. Sedna. Tangaroa.
Ondine. Iara. Ganga. Ryn—

*He stops. So does she. At the end of the rank there is one more board, turned to face
the wall — a black rectangle with no name on it at all.*

**REN (age 9)** *(the way children ask what adults have agreed not to)*: Why is that
one backwards?

*She crouches. We still don't see her face — only the cloth, wiping dust from the
turned board's edge with real care, the way you'd tend a grave you were angry at.*

**WOMAN:** Because they went down and did not come back up. *(beat)* We don't say the
name in this house. We turned the board instead. It's tidier than shame.

**REN (age 9):** …What if they come back?

**WOMAN** *(the first honest pause of the scene)*: Then the board turns itself.
*(rising, moving on)* It never has.

*Hold on the boy alone with the blank board a beat longer than she meant him to be. He
puts a small hand flat against the black lacquer. He does not turn it.*

**SCORE:** *the CALL, once, high and thin. The answer refuses to come — a bare fifth
underneath, and nothing else. This is Ren's sound, and it will not resolve for a long
time.*

## ACT ONE (6:00)

**INT. CLUBROOM — MORNING**

*Episode two's registration chart still hangs by the noticeboard — four names, one
lonely filled square under Kai's. Beside it, brand new: graph paper hand-ruled by
someone with a ruler and a grudge against wobbly lines. At the top, in Mirei's block
capitals: **THE LADDER.** Down the left, a column of seconds — 3, 5, 8, 12, 20. Across
the top, dates running out past the end of term. Every square is empty. GOUDA in front
of it with instant coffee. BO vibrating. MIREI holding the marker. KAI looking at the
3 at the bottom of the column like it was left there to insult him.*

**GOUDA:** Three seconds.

**KAI:** …That's the whole plan.

**GOUDA:** That's today's plan. Then five. Then eight. Nobody skips a rung, and nobody
gets a bonus for feeling brave that afternoon.

**BO** *(entirely sincere)*: I love it. It's like a ladder!

**MIREI** *(flat)*: It is a ladder. It's called The Ladder.

**BO:** I know! That's why I love it!

*Kai is still looking at the 3. Gouda watches him look at it.*

**GOUDA** *(quieter, not softer)*: You want to say it's small. Say it.

**KAI:** …It's small.

**GOUDA:** Good. It's supposed to be. *(beat)* Tuesday I put you in water because a
number in a basin made me hopeful. That's the last time anything in your training
happens because an adult got hopeful. Small is the only thing that's ever worked, for
anybody, in the history of this sport. *(he holds up three fingers — not a lecture, a
list)* And the rules go on the chart, not in your memory. One: never a rung without a
buddy in arm's reach who isn't holding their own breath. Two: no big fast breaths
beforehand, ever — that's how people go out on the way up and never feel it coming.
Three: first twitch, first shake, anything strange in your hands, we're done. Not the
rep. The *session.*

**MIREI** *(already writing them along the chart's bottom margin)*: They're on the
chart now. That means they're real.

**BO** *(to Kai)*: My dad's rules, basically. Except with handwriting.

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — AFTER SCHOOL**

*The after-hours slot, deck empty, water flat and gold-green. Positions exactly as
drilled: BO in the shallow end at arm's length in the club T-shirt he always swims in,
feet planted, hands free. MIREI on deck with the stopwatch and pulse readout, glasses
fogging in the humidity — she wipes them on her sleeve and puts them straight back on
without once leaving the clock. GOUDA crouched at the gutter, immovable.*

**BO** *(routine, checking anyway, every time)*: Shallow end. Feet down. Hands on the
gutter. You can stand up out of this any second you want.

**KAI:** I know.

**BO:** I know you know. I say it anyway. Dad still says it to me and I've been doing
this since I was four.

*Kai takes the gutter. The shell-cord bracelet slides down over his knuckles; he
pushes it back up his wrist without thinking, the way he always has. Three slow
breaths — in through the nose, long easy exhale — unhurried, nothing dramatic. He
lowers his face.*

**UNDERWATER — THREE SECONDS.** *Chlorine sting. Sun through the surface in broken
plates. His eyes are squeezed shut, hard.*

**SCORE:** *the Dive Reflex motif — the heartbeat easing, unhurried, 72 down toward
38. Warm. Safe. Allowed to settle all the way, this time.*

*He comes up. Breathing too fast — and standing, and he did not thrash.*

**MIREI:** Three point one.

*Nobody cheers. Bo, magnificently, does not cheer — he nods once, a man confirming the
tide came in on schedule. Gouda's face does nothing at all, which is its own applause.*

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — SERIES OF DAYS (MONTAGE, 0:45)**

*No swell, no triumph — the training bill paid in the dullest available currency:
three seconds, three seconds, three seconds, and Mirei's neat little **3.1 / 3.0 /
3.4.** Rain one day; they do it in the rain, Gouda under a hood, nobody mentions it.
Bo counting on his fingers, watching Kai's face and never his own watch. Five seconds.
Eyes still shut. Five seconds again. One rep, Kai comes up at a second and a half —
Gouda says nothing and writes nothing, and the square stays empty. Kai looks at that
empty square longer than he ever looked at a filled one.*

*And in the hallway, a crowd at the sports noticeboard:* **ALL-YEAR SWIM TRIAL —
FRIDAY LUNCH — OPEN TO SPECTATORS,** *and one name circled twice in pen —* **KUROSE,
R. — 50 FREE.** *Kai slows just enough to read it; he doesn't know why the name
catches. Behind him, unseen, Ren reads the same sheet from the far side of the crowd,
watching Kai walk away from it.*

## ACT TWO (6:30)

**EXT. POOL — FRIDAY — LUNCH**

*Packed. Fence three deep, phones out, the carnival energy of a school handed a
celebrity for forty minutes. MIREI has set up at the timing end like it's a real meet,
because she is incapable of watching a race without recording it. Bo holds fence space
for Kai, who didn't ask and is standing in it. GOUDA at the back against the equipment
shed, not watching the crowd — watching the water. REN steps onto the block in school
jammer, school cap, borrowed goggles, and looks for the first time all week entirely
at home.*

**THE START.** *The buzzer. His reaction isn't fast so much as* already decided: *off
the block before the sound finishes being a sound, into the water in a line so clean
the surface barely acknowledges being broken.*

**UNDERWATER.** *And here the show whispers. Streamline. Dolphin kick from the hips,
five beats, six — and the water in front of him is not doing what water does. It isn't
piling. It isn't resisting. It is getting out of the way slightly before it has been
asked to. The wake behind him snaps shut with a hard clean crack the deck feels in its
feet before it hears. And for one frame — one, and nobody at that fence will ever
prove it — his eyes behind the goggles are not silver-gray. They are cold Kuroshio
silver: the color of a current agreeing with him.*

**SCORE:** *the Sunlit stinger. D5, bright, close, full attack — the ladder's bottom
rung, sounded once, so the audience knows the show has just quietly put a number on
him.*

*He breaks out at the fifteen-meter mark. Exactly at it. Not a hand past the legal
line, because in front of a hundred and fifty phones this boy will not give away a
centimeter a judge could take back. The sound the crowd makes isn't a cheer — it's the
noise a room makes when it realizes it isn't qualified to be watching. The turn is a
crash-flip so tight it barely registers as an event, then twenty-five meters of a boy
going through water like water is a formality. Touch.*

**MIREI** *(staring at her own stopwatch)*: …Twenty-three, four.

**BOY #2** *(from episode two)*: What's that mean?

**MIREI** *(not unkindly, still staring)*: It means the pool's too small for him and he
did it anyway.

*Ren pulls out on his arms, streaming, and does not look at the crowd. While a hundred
and fifty people applaud he watches the water settle back down in his lane — checking
it, the way a man checks whether he left a mark. At the back, Gouda hasn't moved. But
his coffee is at his side, forgotten, and he is looking at that lane too. He saw the
wake snap. He knows exactly what he saw.*

**GOUDA** *(under his breath, no warmth in it)*: …In a school pool. In front of
everybody.

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — TWENTY MINUTES LATER**

*The crowd has drained back to class; the deck is wet and littered and quiet. In the
corner of the shallow end the swim club is doing its ordinary business, because Friday
is a rung day and rung days don't move for anyone. Kai on the gutter, Bo at arm's
length, Mirei with the watch. Very small, very unglamorous, no audience left at all —
except one. REN, dressed again in the black House Kurose tracksuit with its small
silver wave-and-torii crest, stands at the fence with a towel around his neck, and
watches.*

**MIREI:** Eight seconds. Ready when you are.

*Three slow breaths. Kai lowers his face.*

**UNDERWATER — EIGHT SECONDS.** *Eyes shut. Fists on the rail. Eight is longer than it
sounds. His heartbeat comes up under the mix — steady, honest, working.*

*He surfaces, wipes his face, breathing hard.*

**MIREI:** Eight point two. Clean.

**BO:** Rung's a rung. That's the day.

*And from the fence, flat, at the precise volume of a boy who wants to be heard and
doesn't want to have raised his voice:*

**REN:** Eight seconds.

*Everyone turns. Bo's whole posture changes — he moves half a step so he is between
the fence and Kai without appearing to have moved at all.*

**REN** *(cont'd, coming to the gate, unhurried)*: I've watched four days. Monday it
was three. *(beat)* You understand that a child does eight seconds. A child does eight
seconds in a bath.

**KAI** *(evenly, still in the water)*: Yeah.

*That "yeah" lands wrong for Ren. He came for a flinch. He adjusts.*

**REN:** They tell a story where I'm from. About a line of swimmers who went down. Not
down like depth — down like *out.* Down and gone, and everyone else has been
apologizing for them ever since. *(taking in the eight-second corner of the pool, the
towel, the chart propped against the fence)* And here's the whole inheritance.
Drowning by inches. On purpose. On a Friday afternoon.

**BO** *(no longer cheerful, which from him is genuinely alarming)*: That's enough.

**REN** *(ignoring him entirely, to Kai)*: Do you even know what your father was?

*The deck goes very quiet. Mirei's pen has stopped.*

**KAI:** He was the best in the world at what he did. Everyone knows that.

**REN** *(quietly — and it isn't a taunt, it's the most honest thing he says all
episode)*: That's what everyone knows.

*He lets it sit one second too long and turns to go — and Kai, chest-deep in three
feet of water with his hair dripping into his eyes, says the thing that stops him.*

**KAI:** You're right. Three seconds Monday. Eight today.

**REN** *(stopping)*: …That's your answer.

**KAI:** That's my week. *(beat, no heat in it at all)* Tomorrow it's twelve.

*Ren looks at him. Really looks — and something behind his face doesn't work. Contempt
is a tool that needs shame to land on, and there isn't any here. His eyes go, for half
a beat, to the water around Kai. Checking it. Reading it. He came down here to see
whether water behaves differently near this boy. It doesn't. It's pool water — flat,
chlorinated, indifferent, eight seconds deep.*

**REN** *(finally, and he has nothing better)*: …Twelve.

*He goes, and doesn't look back, which costs him something.*

**BO:** Okay, so I hate him.

**MIREI** *(writing, dead flat)*: Noted in the log.

**KAI** *(quiet, watching the gate swing shut)*: He wasn't looking at me. At the end.
He was looking at the *water.*

*Bo and Mirei glance at each other. Neither has an answer. Kai lets it go — but not
quite, and the camera holds on his face one beat past comfortable.*

## ACT THREE (5:30)

**EXT. POOL — DUSK, THE SAME DAY**

*Deck lights off. The gate's padlock hangs open on its hasp — unlocked, deliberately,
by someone who left before anyone arrived. KAI stands at the gutter in the near-dark,
bag down, already changed. Alone. Twelve seconds is tomorrow. Nobody is here. Nobody
would ever know if tonight it was twelve. He crouches, puts a hand flat on the wet
tile lip, breathes in —*

*— and stops. He looks at the empty deck: the spot where Bo stands, the spot where
Mirei stands, the gutter where Gouda plants himself and doesn't move. Three empty
spots.*

**KAI** *(out loud, mostly annoyed about it)*: …Rule one before rule one.

*He picks his bag back up and goes.*

**INTERCUT — INT. FACULTY OFFICE — CONTINUOUS**

*Gouda at the window, jacket on, keys in hand, watching the dark deck below. He watches
Kai walk away from the water; his grip on the keys eases. A minute later he watches
Kai come back — with Bo lumbering alongside, still chewing something from the fish
stall, gesturing enormously about nothing. Two figures on the deck. Not one. Gouda
sets the keys on the sill, takes his jacket off, and sits back down where he can still
see the window.*

**GOUDA** *(to the empty office)*: …Fine.

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — NIGHT**

*One low bank of lights. The water is black-green and absolutely still. Bo in the
shallows in his club shirt, feet planted, hands free, having asked exactly zero
questions about being fetched from dinner.*

**BO:** Twelve's tomorrow. Chart says twelve's tomorrow.

**KAI:** I know what the chart says.

**BO** *(a beat; then, easily)*: Okay. Then I'm counting, and I'm calling it, and when
I call it you come up. That's the deal or I go finish my dinner.

**KAI:** Deal.

*Kai takes the gutter. Three slow breaths — no more, no faster, exactly as taught. Bo
watches Kai's face, not a watch. Kai lowers his face into black water.*

**UNDERWATER.** *Eight seconds. Eyes shut. His pulse in the mix, slow and honest.
Nine. Ten. Twelve — the rung — and he does not come up. Fourteen. And behind his
eyelids the thing that always comes: moonlight in shafts, black water below, a vast
patient light going out—*

**SCORE:** *the Dive Reflex motif deforms — the same figure, sped up and clipped at
the edges. The show's safe sound going wrong. Panic, on schedule.*

*His hands tighten on the rail. His chest starts to lock. Everything in him gets ready
to thrash. And instead — because he has done a small boring stupid thing three seconds
at a time for eleven straight days, and because his body has finally run out of
reasons to believe this particular three feet of water is the night sea —*

*— he opens his eyes.*

*And it's a swimming pool. Tile. A drain cover. The lane line's floats, blurred and
enormous this close. His own dark hands on the rail. Bo's legs, an arm's length away,
planted like pilings. One low lamp lying across the bottom in a wobbling stripe. No
moonlight. No shafts. No eye. No cathedral hush, no gathering, no vast gentle tone.
Nothing mythic in this frame at all — the pool refuses, for once, to be anything but a
pool. And Kai Isozaki, fifteen years old, looks at it.*

**SCORE:** *the CALL — and the answer comes back from below a beat and a half early.
Nobody on this deck can hear it. The audience is not told what it means. It arrives
before it should, and moves on.*

*Sixteen seconds. Eighteen. On the surface, Bo's fingers counting, unhurried — and
then not unhurried.*

**BO:** Kai. Up. Now — up.

*Muffled, warm, completely clear. Kai pushes off the rail. He comes up at twenty-one
seconds, not thrashing and not calm — grinning, actually grinning, a thing this show
has not once seen him do — and then the deck tilts. The grin slides off. He grabs for
the gutter and misses by a hand's width. Bo's arm is around him before he's finished
missing.*

**BO:** Whoa. Sit. Sit *down*, big man, right here—

*Bo has him up on the step and sitting in a second and a half. Kai's fingertips are
tingling. The lights have halos. His breathing is fast and shallow.*

**KAI** *(thin)*: 'M fine. It's — it's just—

**BO:** Head down. Breathe out slow, long as you can make it. Out, not in. In's the
easy part.

*He does. Once, twice. The halos shrink; the deck stops moving. It takes about forty
unglamorous seconds and the show spends every one of them. Then Bo — who has never
once been angry on screen — sits back on his heels, and his face is completely
different.*

**BO:** That's the only time I'm ever going to be mad at you.

**KAI:** …Bo—

**BO:** No, you listen to this bit, and then we're fine. *(steady, not loud at all)*
You went past the rung. In the dark, with your face in the water, nine seconds past.
If I hadn't been stood in it and you'd gone another twenty and gone quiet — nobody
goes *loud*, Kai. That's the whole thing about it. My dad's had two mates go quiet and
neither of them looked like anything was happening. They just stopped.

*Silence. The water laps.*

**KAI** *(genuinely)*: …I'm sorry.

**BO:** Yeah. *(and just like that the storm is gone)* Good. It goes on the chart, and
Gouda says something in the morning that makes us both want to be somewhere else.
That's the price. You paid it. You're fine.

*He sits down properly on the step, dripping, shoulder to shoulder, looking at the
black water with him. A long easy quiet. And then, making nothing of it, in the voice
he'd use to report the weather:*

**BO** *(cont'd)*: Your eyes were open.

*Kai goes very still.*

**KAI** *(carefully, like it might come apart if he handles it wrong)*: …Yeah.

**BO:** Yeah.

*That's the whole acknowledgment. Bo stands, grunts, retrieves Kai's towel from the
rail and drops it on his head from above, blinding him.*

**BO** *(cont'd, entirely normal again)*: Mum's got the second batch on. If we run we
get it hot.

*Kai pulls the towel down and looks, once more, at the water. Not at a monster. Not at
a grave. At a pool, with a bottom he has now personally seen with his own two eyes. He
gets up, and they run.*

**END CARD BEAT — INTERCUT**

**INT. CLUBROOM — NIGHT**

*MIREI alone at the wall chart, coat still on, having come back for this. THE LADDER:
eleven days of small filled squares climbing out of the corner — and above the row
marked 8, one new square filled in alone, higher than anything before it, a careful
asterisk beside it and a note in her tiny letters:*

> ***0:21 — unscheduled. Buddy present. Post-rep dizziness, recovered. Rung order to
> be respected. Eyes open (B. confirms).***

*The whole climb, three to twenty-one, would fit inside a matchbox; above it the empty
squares go on for months. She sets the private notebook open beside it — episode
three's off-the-axis page, the sentence she wrote and didn't cross out — and looks
from one to the other. Two records of the same boy that do not describe the same boy.*

**MIREI** *(murmured, to nobody)*: One of you is lying.

*She doesn't decide which. She closes the private one, squares the chart on the wall
until it's straight, and turns off the light.*

**INTERCUT — INT. A PRIVATE LAP POOL — SAME NIGHT**

*The immaculate empty training pool from episode three. The silent HOUSE ATTENDANT in
Kurose blacks at the wall with the stopwatch, because nobody swims unwatched, not even
here, not even him. REN swims fifties. Touch, check the attendant's hand for the
number, push off, swim another — the same distance he already owns, faster than any
boy at Munakata will swim in his life, and none of it is what he is chasing. Between
two of them he stops at the wall and holds up three fingers to himself under the water
where the attendant can't see. Then five. Then eight. He looks at his own hand like it
belongs to someone he doesn't respect and can't stop thinking about. Then he swims
another fifty.*

**SCORE:** *the CALL again, from the cold open, over both pools. Two boys, both
counting. The answer still does not come for Ren. Hold on this a beat longer than
feels comfortable.*

**CUT TO BLACK. CREDITS.**

## POST-CREDITS (0:30)

**INT. LISTENING STATION — LOCATION UNKNOWN — NIGHT**

*The same cold room. The TECHNICIAN has rigged a second drum beside the first and
patched both into an old brass-throated speaker — the first time in three episodes
anyone has thought to* listen *to the thing instead of measuring it. She throws the
switch. The room fills with it: a low figure, falling — and beneath it, an answer,
arriving a beat and a half before it has any business arriving. She has spent fifteen
years calling that second line an echo, and no echo in the world has ever gotten
anywhere early.*

*She lifts the phone with no dial.*

**TECHNICIAN:** It isn't one source. It's two, and they're — *(choosing the word)* —
answering each other. I need the sealed file. The fifteen-year entry. The one signed—

*A VOICE on the other end. Male. Calm. Utterly unhurried. We don't hear the words —
only the shape of them, short and final. Her jaw sets.*

**TECHNICIAN** *(flat)*: …Understood. Retired designation. Log it and leave it.

*She hangs up and looks at the drum, where the two lines keep tracing each other in
the dark. She does not log it.*

**SMASH TO BLACK.**

## Writer's notes for draft 2 (things to decide on the redline)

1. **The cold-open name-board corridor** — seeds Ren's inheritance without naming the
   Meridian, the Currents, or the Isozaki line out loud. Option: cut the recitation to
   three names and a silence — colder, better on rewatch, at the cost of the "ten
   Houses" shape being legible this early.
2. **The unnamed woman** — shot shoulders-down and never named, so Ren's mother can be
   introduced properly when the House is (beat sheet, eps 11–12). Option: make her an
   elder or tutor, so the corridor implies nothing about which adult taught him this.
3. **Ren's demonstration fifty at whisper level** — the Surface Form played almost
   entirely as real swimming, with two mythic tells only (the wake snapping shut; one
   frame of Kuroshio silver) and no named technique, because a sanctioned school trial
   is the shallowest water the show owns. Option: drop the eye frame and let the wake
   carry it — which also settles episode three's redline note 3 the other way.
4. **Kai going past the rung** — the episode's one dangerous act, staged with a buddy
   in arm's reach, a hard call-up, an honest bill (dizziness, tingling hands, forty
   seconds on the step) and Bo's single flash of anger. Option: cut the dizziness and
   let Bo's speech carry it; the counter-argument is that an unpaid twenty-one seconds
   would be the show's first free power-up in four episodes.
5. **No domestic beat this episode** — the grandmother's table is deliberately absent,
   because the beat sheet reserves the next one for episode 5's Noah story and because
   Kai's win is one he keeps to himself. Option: restore a half-page kitchen scene
   where he says "nothing much" and she reads his back anyway — warmer, at the cost of
   the act ending on Bo's "yeah."
