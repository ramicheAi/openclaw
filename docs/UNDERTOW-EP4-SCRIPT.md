## COLD OPEN (2:30)

**INT. A HOUSE KUROSE HALL — LACQUER CORRIDOR — SEVEN YEARS AGO**

*Not a training hall. A corridor — long, dim, immaculate, smelling of cedar and cold
stone. Along one wall, mounted in ranks like a library of oars: lacquered NAME-BOARDS,
black, each cut with a single family name in silver. Dozens of them. Generations.*

*A BOY of nine walks the corridor with his hands at his sides because he has been
told to walk with his hands at his sides. REN, before the fade, before the posture
became armor — but already, unmistakably, the same face.*

*Ahead of him, a WOMAN moves at a pace he has to work to match. We see her only from
behind and only from the shoulders down: a plain dark sleeve, one hand carrying a
cloth. She is not introduced. She will not be, this season.*

**WOMAN** *(not slowing)*: Read them.

**REN (age 9)** *(dutiful, reciting as he walks)*: Kurose. Thalassa. Yemoja. Sedna.
Tangaroa. Ondine. Iara. Ganga. Ryn—

*He stops. So does she. At the end of the rank there is one more board, and it has
been turned to face the wall. Only its blank lacquered back shows — a black rectangle
with no name on it at all.*

**REN (age 9)** *(cont'd, the way children ask the question adults have decided not
to)*: Why is that one backwards?

*The woman crouches. We still don't see her face — only the cloth, which she uses to
wipe the dust from the turned board's edge with real care, the way you'd tend a grave
you were angry at.*

**WOMAN:** Because they went down and they did not come back up. *(beat)* We don't
say the name in this house. We turned the board instead. It's tidier than shame.

**REN (age 9):** …What if they come back?

*A pause. The first honest pause of the scene.*

**WOMAN:** Then the board turns itself. *(she rises, moving on)* It never has.

*Hold on the boy, alone with the blank board a beat longer than she meant him to be.
He reaches out — small hand, flat against black lacquer — and does not turn it.*

**SCORE:** *the CALL, once, high and thin. The answer refuses to come; a bare fifth
underneath it and nothing else. This is Ren's sound and it will not resolve for a
long time.*

**SMASH CUT TO:**

**INT. MUNAKATA HIGH SCHOOL — CHANGING ROOM — PRESENT — MORNING**

*A locker door closes. REN KUROSE, 16, in the school's plain gray PE kit rather than
his own blacks, checks nothing in the mirror because there is nothing to check. Beyond
the door, the wet echo of a school pool filling with a hundred and fifty teenagers.*

*He flexes his hand once — the same hand, seven years older — and goes out.*

## ACT ONE (6:00)

**INT. CLUBROOM — MORNING, BEFORE FIRST PERIOD**

*Cramped, cheerful, smelling of chlorine and old kickboards. The registration chart
from episode two is still pinned by the noticeboard — four names, one lonely filled
square under Kai's.*

*Beside it, brand new: a second sheet of graph paper, hand-ruled by someone with a
ruler and a grudge against wobbly lines. At the top, in Mirei's neat block capitals:
**THE LADDER.** Down the left, a column of seconds — 3, 5, 8, 12, 20 — and across the
top, dates, running out past the end of term.*

*Every square is empty.*

*GOUDA stands in front of it with a mug of instant coffee, having clearly been in the
building longer than any teenager would believe. BO is already vibrating. MIREI has
the marker. KAI stands with his arms crossed, looking at the number 3 at the bottom of
the column like it's been left there to insult him.*

**GOUDA:** Three seconds.

**KAI:** …That's the whole plan.

**GOUDA:** That's today's plan. Then it's five. Then eight. Nobody skips a rung and
nobody gets a bonus for feeling brave that afternoon. *(taps the chart)* This is the
plan for as long as the plan takes.

**BO** *(delighted, entirely sincere)*: I love it. It's like a ladder!

**MIREI** *(flat)*: It is a ladder. It's called The Ladder.

**BO:** I know! That's why I love it!

*Kai doesn't laugh. He's still looking at the 3. Gouda watches him look at it.*

**GOUDA** *(quieter, not softer)*: You want to say it's small. Say it.

**KAI:** …It's small.

**GOUDA:** Good. It's supposed to be. *(beat)* Friday I put you in water because a
number in a basin made me hopeful. That's the last time anything about your training
happens because an adult got hopeful. Small is how it's actually done. Small is the
only thing that's ever worked, for anybody, in the whole history of this sport.

*He sets the mug down and holds up three fingers — not a lecture, a list.*

**GOUDA** *(cont'd)*: Rules go on the chart, not in your memory. One: you never do a
rung without a buddy in arm's reach who is not holding their own breath. Two: no big
fast breaths beforehand, ever — that's how people go out on the way up and never feel
it coming. Three: first sign of a twitch, a shake, anything strange in your hands,
the session's over. Not the rep. The *session.*

**MIREI** *(already writing them along the chart's bottom margin in small, exact
letters)*: Those are on the chart now. That means they're real.

**BO** *(cheerfully, to Kai)*: My dad's rules, basically. Except with handwriting.

*Mirei produces, without ceremony, a folded club-issue swimsuit and drops it on the
table in front of Kai — actual Munakata jammers, club colors, not the borrowed pair
he's been living in since episode one.*

**MIREI** *(not looking up)*: Requisition form. The school owed us four sets and only
ever sent three, which I noticed, because I notice things.

*Kai picks them up. Something goes across his face that he doesn't have a word for
yet, and Mirei has already moved on, which is the kindest thing she could possibly do.*

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — AFTER SCHOOL**

*Gates open, lights on, deck deliberately empty — the after-hours slot again. The
water is flat and gold-green in the late sun.*

*Positions, exactly as drilled: BO in the shallow end at arm's length, in the club
T-shirt he always swims in, feet planted, hands free. MIREI on the deck with the
stopwatch and the pulse clip's readout, her glasses fogging in the humidity — she
wipes them on her sleeve and puts them straight back on without breaking eye contact
with the clock. GOUDA crouched at the gutter, immovable.*

**BO** *(routine, checking anyway, every time)*: Shallow end. Feet down. Hands on the
gutter. You can stand up out of this any second you want to.

**KAI:** I know.

**BO:** I know you know. I say it anyway. *(grins)* Dad says it to *me* and I've been
doing this since I was four.

*Kai takes the gutter in both hands. Three slow breaths — in through the nose, long
easy exhale — the ritual from the dock, unhurried, nothing dramatic.*

**GOUDA:** Three-count. That's the whole ask. Nothing brave.

*Kai lowers his face.*

**UNDERWATER — THREE SECONDS.** *Chlorine sting. Sun coming through the surface in
broken plates. His eyes are squeezed shut, hard.*

**SCORE:** *the Dive Reflex motif — the heartbeat easing, unhurried, 72 down toward
38. Warm. Safe. It is allowed to settle all the way this time.*

*He comes up. Water running off his chin. He is breathing too fast, and he is
standing, and he did not thrash.*

**MIREI:** Three point one.

*Nobody cheers. Bo, magnificently, does not cheer — he just nods, once, like a man
confirming the tide came in on schedule. Gouda's face does nothing at all, which is
its own kind of applause.*

**GOUDA:** Again. Two minutes' rest, and rest means rest — sit down.

*Kai sits on the step. His hands are shaking slightly. He looks at them, then at the
water, then at Bo, who has already started telling a story about an eel.*

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — SERIES OF DAYS (MONTAGE, 0:45)**

*No music swell, no triumph — the show's promise about training bills, paid in the
dullest possible currency:*

- *Three seconds. Three seconds. Three seconds.*
- *Mirei filling squares. Her marker's neat little **3.1 / 3.0 / 3.4.***
- *Rain one day; they do it in the rain, Gouda under a hood, nobody mentions it.*
- *Bo counting on his fingers, watching Kai's face and never his own watch.*
- *Five seconds. Kai's eyes still shut. Five seconds again.*
- *One rep where Kai comes up too early, at one and a half, and Gouda says nothing at
  all and writes nothing at all, and the square stays empty. Kai stares at the empty
  square longer than he stared at any of the filled ones.*

**INT. HALLWAY — DAY**

*A crowd at the sports noticeboard. A printed sheet: **ALL-YEAR SWIM TRIAL — FRIDAY
LUNCH — 25M POOL — OPEN TO SPECTATORS.** Ordinary school business, except for the
name someone has already circled twice in pen in the entry column.*

**KUROSE, R. — 50 FREE.**

**BOY #1** *(from episode two, breathless)*: He's actually doing it. In our pool. In
*our* pool.

**BOY #2:** My brother says he went 23-low at Nationals.

**BOY #1:** In a *tech suit*, though.

**BOY #2:** Yeah, well. He's going to do it Friday in a *school jammer* and you're
going to cry.

*Kai, passing behind them with his bag, slows just enough to read the sheet. He
doesn't know why the name catches. He keeps walking. Behind him, unseen, Ren is
reading the same sheet from the other side of the crowd — and watching Kai walk away
from it.*

## ACT TWO (6:30)

**EXT. POOL — FRIDAY — LUNCH**

*Packed. The fence three deep, the deck lined, phones out, the specific carnival
energy of a school that has been handed a celebrity for forty minutes. Teachers have
given up on the crowd control and are simply spectating.*

*MIREI has set up at the timing end like it's a real meet, clipboard braced, because
she is physically incapable of watching a race without recording it. Bo has claimed
fence space with his arms out to hold a gap for Kai, who did not ask for it and is
standing in it.*

*GOUDA is at the back, against the equipment shed, arms folded. Not watching the
crowd. Watching the water.*

*REN steps up onto the block. School jammer, school cap, borrowed goggles. He looks,
for the first time all week, entirely at home.*

**MIREI** *(to herself, thumb on the watch)*: Come on then.

**THE START.** *The buzzer. Ren's reaction is not fast so much as* already decided —
*he is off the block before the sound has finished being a sound, and he enters the
water in a line so clean the surface barely acknowledges being broken.*

**UNDERWATER.** *And here the show whispers.*

*Streamline. Dolphin kick from the hips, five beats, six — and the water in front of
him is not doing what water does. It is not piling. It is not resisting. It is simply
getting out of the way slightly before it has been asked to. The wake behind him
snaps shut with a hard, clean crack the deck feels in its feet before it hears.*

*For one frame — one, and no viewer at the fence will ever be able to prove it — his
eyes behind the goggles are not silver-gray. They are cold Kuroshio silver, the color
of a current that has agreed with him.*

**SCORE:** *the Sunlit stinger. D5, bright, close, full attack — the ladder's bottom
rung, sounded so the audience knows the show has just quietly put a number on him.*

*He breaks out at the 15-meter mark. Exactly at it. Not a hand past the legal line —
because at a school trial, in front of a hundred and fifty phones, this boy will not
give a single centimeter that a judge could take back.*

**ON THE DECK.** *The sound the crowd makes isn't a cheer. It's the noise a room makes
when it realizes it's watching something it's not qualified to watch.*

*The turn: a crash-flip so tight it barely registers as an event. Second length.
Twenty-five meters of a boy going through water like the water is a formality.*

*Touch.*

**MIREI** *(staring at her own stopwatch)*: …Twenty-three, four.

**BOY #2:** What's that mean?

**MIREI** *(not unkindly, still staring)*: It means the pool's too small for him and
he did it anyway.

*Ren pulls himself out on his arms, streaming, and does not look at the crowd. He
takes a towel from a rack, and while a hundred and fifty people applaud him he
watches the water settle back down in his lane, checking it — the way a man checks
whether he left a mark.*

*At the back, Gouda has not moved. But his coffee is at his side, forgotten, and he is
looking at the lane's surface too. He saw the wake snap. He knows exactly what he
saw.*

**GOUDA** *(under his breath, to no one)*: …In a school pool. In front of everybody.
*(beat, and there's no warmth in it)* His mother would skin him.

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — SAME DAY, TWENTY MINUTES LATER**

*The crowd has drained back to class. The deck is wet and littered and quiet. In the
shallow end, in the corner, the swim club is doing its ordinary business, because
Friday is a rung day and rung days don't move for anyone.*

*Kai on the gutter. Bo at arm's length. Mirei with the watch. It is very small and
very unglamorous and there is no audience left at all.*

*Except one.*

*REN, dressed again, black House Kurose tracksuit over school trousers — the small
silver wave-and-torii crest at the chest — stands at the fence with a towel around his
neck and watches.*

**MIREI:** Eight seconds. Ready when you are.

*Kai breathes. Three slow. Lowers his face.*

**UNDERWATER — EIGHT SECONDS.** *Eyes shut. Fists on the gutter rail. Eight is longer
than it sounds. His heartbeat comes up under the mix, steady, honest, working.*

*He surfaces. Wipes his face. Breathing hard.*

**MIREI:** Eight point two. Clean.

**BO:** Rung's a rung. That's the day.

*And from the fence, flatly, with the precise volume of a boy who wants to be heard
and doesn't want to have raised his voice:*

**REN:** Eight seconds.

*Everyone turns. Bo's whole posture changes — he moves half a step so he's between
the fence and Kai without appearing to have moved at all.*

**REN** *(cont'd, coming to the gate, unhurried)*: I've been watching for four days.
Monday it was three. *(beat)* You understand that a child does eight seconds. A child
does eight seconds in a bath.

**KAI** *(evenly, still in the water)*: Yeah.

*That "yeah" lands wrong for Ren. He came for a flinch. He adjusts.*

**REN:** They tell a story where I'm from. About a line of swimmers who went down.
Not down like depth — down like *out*. Down and gone, and the rest of us have been
apologizing for them ever since. *(he looks at the eight-second corner of the pool,
at the towel, at the chart Mirei has propped against the fence)* And here's the whole
inheritance. Drowning by inches, on purpose, on a Friday afternoon.

**BO** *(no longer cheerful, which is genuinely alarming from him)*: Hey. That's
enough.

**REN** *(ignoring him entirely, to Kai)*: Do you even know what your father was?

*Beat. The deck is very quiet. Mirei's pen has stopped.*

**KAI:** He was the best in the world at what he did. Everyone knows that.

**REN** *(quietly, and it is not a taunt — it is the most honest thing he says all
episode)*: That's what everyone knows.

*He lets that sit exactly one second too long, then turns to go — and Kai, still
standing chest-deep in three feet of water with his hair dripping into his eyes, says
the thing that stops him.*

**KAI:** You're right. Three seconds Monday. Eight today.

**REN** *(stopping)*: …That's your answer.

**KAI:** That's my week. *(beat, no heat in it at all)* Tomorrow it's twelve.

*Ren looks at him. Really looks — and something behind his face doesn't work. Contempt
is a tool that needs shame to land on, and there isn't any here. There's just a boy
in a school pool doing a small thing badly, and saying so.*

*Ren's eyes go, for half a beat, to the water around Kai — checking it. Reading it. He
came down here to see whether the water behaves differently near this boy.*

*It doesn't. It's just pool water. Flat, chlorinated, indifferent, eight seconds deep.*

**REN** *(finally, and he has nothing better)*: …Twelve.

*He goes. He doesn't look back, which costs him something.*

*A silence on the deck. Bo's shoulders come down about an inch.*

**BO:** Okay, so I hate him.

**MIREI** *(writing, dead flat)*: Noted in the log.

**KAI** *(quiet, watching the gate swing shut)*: He wasn't looking at me. At the end.
He was looking at the *water.*

*Bo and Mirei glance at each other. Neither has an answer. Kai lets it go — but he
doesn't quite let it go, and the camera holds on his face one beat past comfortable.*

**INT. CLUBROOM — LATE AFTERNOON**

*Mirei alone, transferring the day's numbers from the clipboard to the wall chart.
Eight point two. She fills the square in.*

*Then she sits back and looks at the whole chart at once — a stubby little staircase
of filled squares climbing out of the bottom-left corner, three to five to eight, with
one gap where a rep didn't count.*

*She takes the private notebook out of her bag. Opens it to the page from episode
three — the off-the-axis data point, the sentence she wrote and didn't cross out.*

*She looks from the private notebook to the public chart and back. Two records of the
same boy that do not describe the same boy at all.*

**MIREI** *(murmured, to nobody)*: One of you is lying.

*She doesn't decide which. She puts the private one away and squares the public one
on the wall, precisely, until it's straight.*

## ACT THREE (5:30)

**EXT. POOL — DUSK, THE SAME DAY**

*The deck lights are off. The gate's padlock hangs open on its hasp — unlocked,
deliberately, by someone who left before anyone arrived.*

*KAI stands at the gutter in the near-dark, bag down, already changed. Alone.*

*He looks at the water for a long moment. Twelve seconds is tomorrow. Nobody is here.
Nobody would ever know if tonight it was twelve.*

*He crouches. Puts a hand flat on the wet tile lip. Breathes in.*

*And stops.*

*He looks at the empty deck — at the spot where Bo stands, at the spot where Mirei
stands, at the gutter where Gouda plants himself and doesn't move. Three empty spots.*

**KAI** *(to himself, out loud, mostly annoyed about it)*: …Rule one before rule one.

*He picks his bag back up and goes.*

**INT. FACULTY OFFICE — CONTINUOUS**

*Gouda at the window with his jacket already on, keys in his hand, watching the dark
pool deck below. He has been standing there long enough for the coffee to go cold.*

*He watches Kai walk away from the water. His grip on the keys eases.*

*Then, a minute later, he watches Kai come back — with Bo lumbering alongside him,
still chewing something from the fish stall, gesturing enormously about nothing.*

*Two figures on the deck. Not one.*

*Gouda watches them take their positions. Then he sets the keys down on the sill,
takes his jacket off, and sits back down at his desk, where he can still see the
window.*

**GOUDA** *(to the empty office)*: …Fine.

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — NIGHT**

*One bank of lights on, low. The water is black-green and absolutely still. Bo, in the
shallows in his club shirt, feet planted, hands free, having asked exactly zero
questions about being fetched from dinner.*

**BO:** Twelve's tomorrow. You know that, right? Chart says twelve's tomorrow.

**KAI:** I know what the chart says.

**BO** *(a beat; then, easily)*: Okay. Then I'm counting, and I'm calling it, and when
I call it you come up. That's the deal or I go finish my dinner.

**KAI:** Deal.

*Kai takes the gutter. Three slow breaths — no more, no faster, exactly as taught.
Bo's eyes are on Kai's face, not on any watch.*

*Kai lowers his face into black water.*

**UNDERWATER.**

*Eight seconds. His eyes are shut. His pulse comes up in the mix, slow and honest.*

*Nine. Ten.*

*Twelve — the rung — and he does not come up.*

*Fourteen. And in the dark behind his eyelids, the thing that always comes: moonlight
in shafts, black water below, a vast patient light going out—*

**SCORE:** *the Dive Reflex motif deforms — the same figure, sped up, clipped at the
edges. The show's safe sound going wrong. Panic, arriving on schedule.*

*His hands tighten on the rail. His chest starts to lock. Everything in him gets
ready to thrash.*

*And instead — because he has done a small stupid boring thing three seconds at a time
for eleven straight days, and because his body has run out of reasons to believe this
particular three feet of water is the night sea —*

*— he opens his eyes.*

*And it's a swimming pool.*

*Tile. A drain cover. The lane line's blue and white floats, blurred and enormous
this close. His own dark hands on the gutter rail. Bo's legs, huge and rippling and
absurd, an arm's length away, planted like pilings. Chlorine stinging, light from one
low lamp lying across the bottom in a wobbling stripe.*

*No moonlight. No shafts. No eye. No cathedral hush, no gathering, no vast gentle
tone. Nothing mythic in this frame at all — the pool refuses, for once, to be
anything but a pool.*

*And Kai Isozaki, fifteen years old, looks at it.*

**SCORE:** *the CALL — and the answer comes back from below a beat and a half early.
Nobody on this deck can hear it. The audience is not told what it means. It simply
arrives before it should, and moves on.*

*Sixteen seconds. Eighteen.*

**ON THE SURFACE.** *Bo's fingers, counting, unhurried, and then not unhurried.*

**BO:** Kai. Up. Now — up.

**UNDERWATER.** *Kai hears it, muffled and warm and completely clear, and pushes off
the rail.*

**EXT. POOL — SHALLOW END — CONTINUOUS**

*He comes up. Twenty-one seconds. He is not thrashing and he is not calm — he is
grinning, actually grinning, which is a thing this show has not once seen him do—*

*—and then the deck tilts.*

*His grin slides off. He grabs for the gutter and misses it by a hand's width. Bo's
arm is around him before he's finished missing.*

**BO:** Whoa. Whoa. Sit. Sit *down*, big man, right here—

*Bo gets him up onto the step and sitting, back against the tile, in about a second
and a half. Kai's fingertips are tingling. The lights have halos. His breathing is
fast and shallow and he can't seem to talk his way out of it.*

**KAI** *(thin)*: 'M fine. It's — it's just—

**BO:** Yeah. Head down. Breathe out slow. Long as you can make it. Out, not in —
in's the easy part.

*He does. Once, twice. The halos shrink. The deck stops moving. It takes about forty
unglamorous seconds, and the show spends every one of them.*

*And then Bo — Bo, who has never once been angry on screen — sits back on his heels
and looks at him, and his face is completely different.*

**BO:** That's the only time I'm ever going to be mad at you.

**KAI:** …Bo—

**BO:** No, you listen to this bit, because I'm only saying it once and then we're
fine. *(steady, and not loud at all)* You went past the rung. You went past it in the
dark, in a pool, with your face in the water, nine seconds past. If I hadn't been
stood in it, and you'd gone another twenty and gone quiet — nobody goes *loud*, Kai.
That's the whole thing about it. My dad's had two mates go quiet and neither of them
looked like anything was happening. They just stopped.

*Silence. The water laps.*

**KAI** *(genuinely)*: …I'm sorry.

**BO:** Yeah. *(and just like that, it's over — the whole storm, gone)* Good. It's
logged. Mirei's going to write it in her tiny letters and Gouda's going to say
something in the morning that makes us both want to be somewhere else. That's the
price. You paid it, you're fine.

*He sits down properly on the step next to Kai, dripping, shoulder to shoulder,
looking out at the black water with him. A long, easy quiet.*

*And then, not making anything of it, in exactly the voice he'd use to report the
weather:*

**BO** *(cont'd)*: Your eyes were open.

*Kai goes very still.*

**KAI** *(carefully, like it might come apart if he handles it wrong)*: …Yeah.

**BO:** Yeah.

*That's it. That's the whole acknowledgment. Bo stands up, grunts, retrieves Kai's
towel from the rail and drops it on his head from above, blinding him.*

**BO** *(cont'd, entirely normal again)*: My mum's got the second batch on. If we run
we get it hot.

*Kai pulls the towel down. He looks — one more time, briefly — back at the water. Not
at a monster and not at a grave. At a pool, with a bottom he has now personally seen
with his own two eyes.*

*He gets up, and they run.*

**INT. KAI'S HOME — KITCHEN — LATER**

*Warm light, low radio, the unhurried rolling bass under a song nobody in this house
has ever named. GRANDMOTHER at the sink. Kai in the doorway, hair still damp, towel
around his neck, smelling of chlorine and grilled fish.*

*She waits. She has, historically, always been given the day.*

**GRANDMOTHER:** Well?

**KAI** *(a beat — and then, choosing it)*: …Nothing much. Practice.

*He goes to wash his hands. And Grandmother, who has raised a boy through eight years
of Nothing Much, stops with a plate in her hand and looks at his back — because that
was not the "nothing much" of a bad day. That was the other kind. The kind you keep.*

*She doesn't ask. She sets an extra piece on his plate instead.*

---

**END CARD BEAT — INTERCUT**

**INT. CLUBROOM — NIGHT — EMPTY**

*Moonlight through the window on the wall chart. THE LADDER. Eleven days of small
filled squares climbing out of the corner — and, above the row marked 8, one new
square filled in, alone, higher than anything before it, with a careful asterisk
beside it and a note in Mirei's tiny letters:*

> ***0:21 — unscheduled. Buddy present. Post-rep dizziness, recovered. Rung order to
> be respected. Eyes open (B. confirms).***

*The chart's whole climb, from 3 to 21, would fit inside a matchbox. Above it, the
empty squares go on for months.*

**INTERCUT — INT. A PRIVATE LAP POOL — SAME NIGHT**

*The immaculate, empty training pool from episode three. The silent HOUSE ATTENDANT
in Kurose blacks at the wall with the stopwatch, because nobody swims unwatched, not
even here, not even him.*

*REN swims fifties. He touches, checks the attendant's hand for the number, pushes off
and swims another. Touch. Number. Again. Touch. Number. Again — the same distance he
already owns, over and over, faster than any boy at Munakata will swim in his life,
and none of them are what he is chasing.*

*Between two of them he stops at the wall, breathing, and holds up three fingers to
himself under the water where the attendant can't see. Then five. Then eight.*

*He looks at his own hand like it belongs to someone he doesn't respect and can't
stop thinking about. Then he pushes off and swims another fifty.*

**SCORE:** *the CALL again, from the cold open, high and thin over both pools. Both
boys, both counting. The answer still does not come for Ren. Hold on this a beat
longer than feels comfortable.*

**CUT TO BLACK. CREDITS.**

## POST-CREDITS (0:30)

**INT. LISTENING STATION — LOCATION UNKNOWN — NIGHT**

*The same cold room. The TECHNICIAN has rigged a second drum beside the first and
patched both into an old brass-throated speaker — the first time in three episodes
anyone has thought to *listen* to the thing instead of measuring it.*

*She throws the switch.*

*And the room fills with it: a low figure, falling — and beneath it, an answer,
arriving a beat and a half before it has any business arriving.*

*She stands very still. She has spent fifteen years calling the second line an echo,
and no echo in the world has ever gotten anywhere early.*

*She lifts the phone with no dial.*

**TECHNICIAN:** It's not one source. It's two, and they're — *(she chooses the word
carefully)* — answering each other. I need the sealed file. The fifteen-year entry.
The one signed—

*A VOICE on the other end. Male. Calm. Utterly unhurried. We do not hear the words —
just the shape of them, short and final.*

*The technician's jaw sets.*

**TECHNICIAN** *(flat)*: …Understood. Retired designation. Log it and leave it.

*She hangs up. She looks at the drum, where the two lines keep tracing each other in
the dark.*

*She does not log it.*

**SMASH TO BLACK.**
